# The 18:00 stand-up of 5 November: what applies here

This is the call where the publication was named (from about 00:25 to 00:52): The Lisbon Wire was dropped (lisbonwire.com exists), "Promptugal" chosen, promptugal.news, .com and .ai bought, the capital T discussed ("No, just the T, just the capital T", 00:49:02), and the audience set: "If you don't get the joke ... this is not for you" (00:57:12).

## 1. Public by default, private in the CRM

- "Anything that we discover publicly is public ... we should only be consuming materials from websites that we can reach without login" (00:00:21 to 00:00:46).
- "Things like an email ... a strategy or ... some names ... We clearly cannot put that online" (00:01:07): those go to the team CRM's private side. The line between this site and the CRM is "the public versus private line" (00:20:02).

## 2. Every claim has a source: our superpower

- "Everything we post on the web has to have a source ... everything should be linked to everything ... where is the source? ... that's our superpower" (00:08:15 to 00:08:54).
- "This only works and this is only gonna be impressive when the width of it is massive" (00:08:54).

## 3. Content, content, content: an article for every storyline

- "Write an article about all of them ... let's not pick one" (00:02:46 to 00:02:59).
- "We need content, content, content ... If you need to spin up three journalists, spin up three journalists ... think of it as a publishing pipeline" (00:20:32). "Your KPI is articles. Just publish as many articles as you can" (to Vicky, 00:53:55).
- Enough material that a visitor finds it interesting; the Portuguese newsroom "never gained escape velocity ... always looked like an amateur-y ... AI bit put together" (00:17:53 to 00:18:15). A homepage "more like a news organization" (00:21:00).

## 4. The roles of a newsroom

- Researcher (finds), curator (maps), journalist (writes and publishes), **correspondent** ("the more senior journalist ... maintains the homepage ... curates"), and the desk ("the overall boss") (00:04:18 to 00:05:01).
- Beat correspondents: "the Brazilian correspondent ... the Web Summit correspondent" (00:05:33); a writer, a translator (00:05:17). The ontologist: "the murder board ... this guy connects to that" (00:09:18).

## 5. Go deep: the Brazilian delegation as the formula

- "If there's 200 Brazilians coming ... this will now be senior people ... I think there's a lot of gold there ... how many stories exist here?" (00:09:48 to 00:10:06).
- "Let's map the universe. Like how many things, how many people, how many companies? And it might be that there isn't a lot of that data out there, which is ... a signal in itself" (00:10:36 to 00:10:44).
- Connect the companies to each other, to the people here, to investors, to talks (00:10:44). "After we've done this for one of these, we then have the formula, and then we just let the agents go around the blocks" (00:11:14).
- Multi-pass, round the clock, as the book's team did: "this agent maps it, and then that finds the evidence, and that one visualizes, and that reviews ... across six hours ... very good explainable data" (00:11:58). "We're gonna find blind spots ... people who reported the wrong thing ... data that didn't update. Maybe there's 300, maybe there's 150" (00:12:20).
- Also with "a lot of good meat": Técnico's robots; less connected: the Gender in Tech report (00:21:19 to 00:21:58).
- Dinis himself goes deep on one area, "like ... the 200 Brazilians or the technical" (00:54:10).

## 6. The measure: visits, referrals, and "can you add more?"

- "One of the most important metrics here is how much visits we can get to the website" (00:12:20). "We know this works if we start to see referrals" (00:22:22).
- The signal: a company answers "can you add more stuff?" rather than "what are you doing?" (00:12:53 to 00:13:27). "It's cheaper for them to ask us ... they will start to link themselves" (00:13:27). Pull requests from people with updates: "How cool would that be?" (00:20:32).
- A service article: "if you're a startup and you wanna know which startups you should meet, come to this website" (00:22:08).

## 7. Outreach: give them something; RiskMandate later

- The email says: "Hey, we just built this for you ... can you come and check it to see if it's correct?" (00:16:19 to 00:16:32).
- "If we try to sell them RiskMandate too soon, they're gonna go ... [no]" (00:16:55). "For every company that we do, we should try to give them something" (00:17:13). A RiskMandate angle on a story, "it's not a headline ... but it's kinda there" (00:19:05).
- Investors: "I want to talk to investors and go, Hey, look, I got a service for you" (00:13:49).

## 8. The summit days, and becoming a publication

- "This is the warmup so that on the summit days ... we won't have time to be maintaining the site" (00:22:39 to 00:22:50): a back-office team or social media manager; whoever comments "this is really cool", ask "do you wanna help?" (00:23:17).
- "Publish up to the last minute" (00:54:40), the lesson of Voice of Europe, where the journalists stopped.
- Social: every article to LinkedIn automatically (00:24:11); accounts under the name (Jose's, 00:54:10).
- A press pass: "we can apply for a press pass" once the site is strong enough, "hopefully by tomorrow or even by Monday ... rich, and professional ... doesn't look like AI slop" (00:45:16, 00:52:44 to 00:53:25); "how to become a publisher in Portugal" (00:52:44).
- Don't make it about Web Summit: careful with the name ("infringing", 00:24:54); "the Web Summit edition" (00:26:41).
