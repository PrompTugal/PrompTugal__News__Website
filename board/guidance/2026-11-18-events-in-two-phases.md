> Dinis, in conductor.b@'s session, 18 November 2026, about 14:15 UTC. Recorded by conductor.b@PrompTugal.dinis; the plan is `board/briefs/2026-11-18-events-discovery-and-the-graph.md`.

> "Why don't we do this in two phases, since we can't publish it, but we can look into it ... use Perplexity for this ... to find out the sources, but also Perplexity should give you lots of links for sources and evidence ... everyone that you have there that points to a source that we don't have is a gap in our sources ... then you connect the dots."

> "We're showing that we have an infrastructure, which is also something that we'll sell ... to almost consume any piece of data ... and more important, make sense of it ... weak links or weak signals ... we know this event happens because of this, this, this, and here's the evidence ... we can't put the details for now, which is also an interesting situation for us to even nudge the organizers to say, hey, can you give us more details because we cannot get them from that source."

> "Do this over time ... past events organized there ... connect this to other people, connect this to the companies ... If somebody ... the author or somebody who actually shares information, can we publish it? ... we're not going to be the only ones with this problem ... local newspapers and local event organizers also ... show the data that we collect, the visualization of the data that we collect, the provenance of the data, where the data is, source, access to the vaults ... So, yeah, let's map it out."

## What follows

1. **Phase 1, look (yes, Perplexity):** per place, ask Perplexity (through OpenRouter, the environment's key, a small budget) what is on; keep every link it cites as evidence. Nothing from it is published as such.
2. **Every cited host we do not read is a gap**, counted on the sources register (how many events point to it); each gap feeds the on-the-record track.
3. **Phase 2, publish what we may:** an event appears with the evidence for it (each source linked, readable or not); its details come only from sources we may read; where they cannot, the page says so and invites the organiser to send them.
4. **The graph over time:** events linked to places, venues, organisers, past editions, companies and people (public and professional only).
5. **Submissions and our own data's reuse** (by an organiser, by a third party; by local papers and organisers) are questions put on the site for Dinis to decide.
6. **The showcase:** the data, its visualisations, its provenance, where it lives (vaults) and how to reach it, shown in full: this is the infrastructure the company sells.
