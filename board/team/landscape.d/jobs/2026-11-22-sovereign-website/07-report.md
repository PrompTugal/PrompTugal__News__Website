Everything here is a reading of public pages on 22 November 2026, each fact with its URL in `../../landscapes/sovereign-website/landscape.json`; absence on a page is not proof of absence in a product; no competitor claim is stated without its source and the date read. Status: first full version, with the re-reads of 22 Nov 18:40 UTC applied (Webflow now read; Zola's licence confirmed as EUPL 1.2; Infomaniak, Hostinger and Cloudflare confirmed).

## In one paragraph
We read 31 players in four rings: six European and sovereignty-led builders, eight mass-market and AI-native builders, seven open-source engines and CMSs with four open-source AI builders, and six hosts. The hosted builders sell the site as a commodity and keep the owner's way out vague: none states an export tool on the pages read, two keep perpetual licences over customer content, and most charge a subscription that renews higher. The open-source engines give the owner the files but ask for a terminal. Nobody read combines files you own, a build by agents for a non-technical owner, edits through your own AI, and a one-off price. That is the room Sovereign Website stands in; it is not yet proven, because nothing has been sold and our own "no technical skill" is not yet true (the owner needs a Claude plan or a partner to edit).

## Where we stand
Strengths: plain files in the owner's repository on any host; one-off price against renewing subscriptions; privacy by default; an open pattern first and a hosted offer second. Weaknesses: nothing sold, no public example, the Claude-plan-or-partner requirement, a price line that differs between sources (500, 250, a 50 proposal), and no sovereignty certification (our plan makes no such claim).
