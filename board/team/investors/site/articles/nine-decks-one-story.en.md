In the past day, a team of AI agents and I made nine slide decks about PrompTugal. Nine is a lot to ask anyone to read, so this is the guide: how they fit together, which one to open first, and the best argument each one makes.

They are all public, as slides and as PDFs, at [promptugal.news/investors/decks](https://promptugal.news/investors/decks/).

## The arc

The decks tell one story in four movements.

**The problem and the answer** (decks 1 and 2). The news industry is looking in the wrong direction. We are not, and what we built is already running.

**The proof** (decks 3 and 9). Not a plan: the product, live, feature by feature, with screenshots of the site as it is today.

**The company** (decks 4 and 5). Where it comes from, the Algarve, in Portugal, in the European Union, and what an investor actually buys.

**How it is possible, and who it needs** (decks 6, 7 and 8). The technology that keeps a team of agents in control, the people we want to join, and an open invitation to copy all of it.

## Deck by deck, with its best argument

**1. The investment case.** The whole story in one deck, if you only read one. Its best line: *others ask people to pay for future value; we let them have the value first.* Readers come for the summit, find the talks they would have missed, and by the time they pay a few cents a page, they are already glad they did.

**2. The industry.** Why news is stuck. "The news industry sells the one thing whose price is going to zero, the article." Its best argument is the bridge, from a simulation I wrote about a fictional town: when a local bridge closes, the paper leads with it five times, but readers need it on fifty-seven days. An article is written once. A maintained claim, kept current, is a service people will pay for, because it saves them time.

**3. The platform.** Every feature, live: sourced and signed articles, the companies at the summit, the graph, search, an assistant that answers from the site, your own day at the summit, a reader's vault, micropayments and an iPhone and iPad app in internal beta. Its best argument is the architecture: no back end ever sees a reader. No IP, no cookie, no profile.

**4. The Algarve.** Why a news company from Loulé. Its best argument is the flywheel: news is how a region gets noticed, and noticed, it grows.

**5. The investment.** A seed round of under €1M buys equity in My Feeds, the company that publishes PrompTugal. Its best line: *we are not raising to start; we are raising to go faster.* It ends with the three questions any investor should ask: can this be a profitable business, can this company be bought in the future, is this the team to execute?

**6. The technology.** About twenty agents for every person, and every one in its lane. Its best argument: *capacity is cheap, control is not.* Adding agents is easy; keeping them named, private and inside a written mandate is what makes this possible, and it is where sgit and RiskMandate come in.

**7. Join us.** For the senior person from the news industry who has wanted to run a newsroom like this for years. It now runs. Come and lead it.

**8. Do it yourself.** The killer deck, and my favourite. Everything is open source, so here are the repositories, the stack and the roadmap. Go and do it. Its best argument: the code is easy to copy; the execution is the moat.

**9. The tour.** Do not take our word for it. A guided walk through the live site, stop by stop, in the order a reader meets it.

## Where to start

- **An investor:** deck 1, then 5, then 9 to check we are real.
- **Someone from the news industry:** deck 2, then 7.
- **A company that could give us a project, or a publisher:** deck 3, then 8.
- **The Algarve's and Portugal's bodies:** deck 4.
- **A builder or a sceptic:** deck 8, then 6.
- **Short on time:** deck 9. It is screenshots.

## Why so many

Because the people we want to hear from are not one audience. An angel investor, a publisher, a journalist, a regional body and a developer each ask a different first question, and each deserves a deck that answers it rather than one that answers everybody's badly.

They were also made the way PrompTugal itself is made: by agents doing the repeatable work, with a person deciding. The site went from its first commit on 4 November 2026 to 426 releases by the 7th. The decks are versioned the same way, and they will change as you tell us what is wrong with them.
