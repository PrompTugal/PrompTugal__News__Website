## 2. What works

1. **The one-way and two-way door split exists and is written down.** CLAUDE.md (23 Nov) and `/modes/` say reversible steps go ahead on the agent's own call and one-way doors come to Dinis. This is the most powerful filter there is; the problem is that it is not applied before an ask is sent (section 3).
2. **Signed records.** An answer becomes a signed, checkable record with its exact scope. That gives accountability and memory, and it is what makes delegation safe.
3. **Panel blocks with "disagreements go to Dinis".** `/blocks/`: a panel weighs a block of items and only a split reaches him. On 24 Nov this cleared most of 31 items without him. This is management by exception and should be the norm.
4. **Quick in-session batches.** When the asks are put in front of him in his own session, short and together, he answers several in one go (several "yes" answers in one wake on 24 and 25 Nov, `from-a.md` lines 183 and 185).
5. **The "Say" line.** The "Now" cards on `/asks/` carry an exact answer to copy ("push 1a72038", "yes to lane.entry/2"). It cuts the cost of answering to one act.
6. **The command centre plan's principles.** Four classes, RACI, "stop is an outcome", briefs by time window: each matches a well-known remedy (section 4). The plan is sound; the risk is in the detail of the cards and the queue.

## 3. What hurts

1. **Volume against attention.** 80 open entries, 267 "Dinis:" lines in status files and asks spread over at least six places (the decisions site, status files, maestro logs, collected lists, a stale librarian list, chat in his sessions). No single queue holds them all, so nobody, Dinis included, can see the whole.
2. **Most asks are not his.** 82% on 23 Nov and about three in four in the later sample were reversible, another agent's, a fact, or a harness prompt. Each one spends his attention, and attention spent on routine makes the one-way doors slower.
3. **The cards lack what a decision needs.** On `/asks/`, 1 of 20 "Now" cards shows a recommendation, none shows a default, 17 have no date and so no age, and only two give a deadline. Without a recommendation he must rebuild the reasoning; without a default the cost of not answering is invisible; without an age nothing is visibly stale.
4. **Bundled, long asks.** Ask lines average about 640 characters and some entries carry several asks in one line (`from-a.md` lines 118, 134, 143). A bundled ask gets one answer for several questions, or none.
5. **Restated, not resolved.** Asks carried across four consecutive wakes (25 Nov, `from-a.md` 186 to 193), a re-sent ask with "no answer in 10 h" (line 134), and status files still asking from 7 and 8 Nov. Repetition without a clock or a default is noise that trains the reader to skim.
6. **Answers that do not land.** "A decided record does not lift a session's own permission check" (`from-a.md` line 136, 24 Nov 01:00 UTC): five agents had a genuine record and still waited; one item was open 24 h 25 min after its decision (line 184). A decision that does not change anything feels, to the decider, like wasted effort, and erodes trust in the channel.
7. **Approval fatigue on security and permission asks.** One ask (D1, 23 Nov) needed the same repository push approval in about ten sessions, one at a time; harness permission prompts were 6 of the 22 asks of 23 Nov; prompts wait in sessions nobody is watching (`escalation.md`). Repeated, near-identical security approvals are exactly the pattern in which people stop reading and start clicking yes.
8. **Escalate, Decide and FYI are mixed.** The running commentary interleaves what is on fire with what is done (Dinis's own point in the plan's preamble), so the urgent competes with the merely new.

## 4. Recommendations (ten, each testable)

Each names the research idea in plain words. I cite no paper here: these are well-established concepts in judgement and decision-making and usable security, named so the team can look them up; I have not re-read a source for this review, so none is cited.

**R1. Triage before it reaches him: a door test on every ask.** Before an ask enters the Decide queue, the asker answers three questions on the card: is it reversible; is it inside my own area; does a rule already settle it. If all three say "mine", it does not go to Dinis; it is done and logged under `exceptions`. *Idea: management by exception; one-way and two-way doors.* *Test:* the share of Decide items that are true one-way doors, now about 18 to 25%; target over 70% within a week.

**R2. Every Decide card shows the recommendation first, and a default with a date.** The card leads with "Recommended: X, because Y (one line)", then "If you do not answer by <date, time>, the default is: <X or not now>". One-way doors default to "not now", never to yes. *Idea: the default effect and status quo bias, used deliberately: a stated default turns silence into a known outcome instead of a stall.* *Test:* the share of asks older than 48 h; target zero without an explicit "not now".

**R3. One question per card, three to five lines, and a single "Say" line.** No bundles: an ask with several questions becomes several cards, grouped. Fields in this order: the ask (one line), recommended answer, what follows yes and what follows no (one line each), the door (one-way or two-way) and who is Accountable, age and deadline, link. *Idea: cognitive load and choice overload: fewer, simpler options are decided faster and better.* *Test:* time from card shown to answer, measured in the app; median under a minute for two-way items.

**R4. Batch the Decide queue into two windows a day; interrupt only for Escalate.** The queue is presented at fixed times (for example morning and evening UTC, at Dinis's choice), sorted by blast radius then age, at most about ten cards per sitting with "show more". Push notifications are for sev1 and sev2 only, as the plan says. *Idea: the cost of interruption and task switching; decision fatigue (the strong "willpower runs out" form is contested in replication, but the practical point holds: quality drops in long unbroken runs of choices, so keep sittings short).* *Test:* pushes per day (target: only sev1 and sev2), and the answered share per sitting.

**R5. "Not now" and "stop here" are first-class answers, each with a consequence.** "Not now" requires a date and moves the card out of sight until then; "stop here" closes the work with "done enough" shown in the Brief's "stopped" list. Neither counts as a failure in any report. *Idea: satisficing (good enough is a legitimate end) and the sunk cost trap (making stopping cheap stops work running on because it was started).* *Test:* count of "not now" and "stop" answers; no card returns before its "not now" date.

**R6. One standing answer per class, not one approval per session.** Where the same approval recurs (a push to the same repository for N sessions, the pinned tool install, signing setup), the card asks once for a scoped standing rule ("all sessions of account D may push their own key commit to IaC, until 30 Nov"), and the escalation table's "standing fix" owner removes the class. *Idea: habituation and approval fatigue in usable security: repeated identical prompts are approved without reading, so each one should be rare and meaningful.* *Test:* number of near-identical asks per week; the D1 pattern (about ten approvals) becomes one.

**R7. Security and one-way asks look different and ask for an act, not a tap.** A one-way door card carries a distinct look, says in one line what cannot be undone, and needs a stronger act (the passkey signature, or typing the short name of the thing), while routine cards are one tap. Never more than a handful of one-way cards per sitting. *Idea: warning design and polymorphic or attention-forcing prompts: a warning that looks like every other dialog is ignored.* *Test:* in the app, zero one-way answers given in under five seconds from showing the card.

**R8. An answer must land, and the card closes the loop.** The card is not done until the asker confirms the effect ("pushed 1a72038, live at v…"); if the answer cannot act (the permission check of 24 Nov), the card comes back once, marked "your answer did not land: one more act needed: <exact act>". *Idea: feedback and closing the loop (people trust and keep using a decision channel when they see each decision take effect).* *Test:* share of answered cards with a confirmed effect within one wake; target over 90%.

**R9. One queue, one count, visible ages; nothing restated.** All asks come from one source (the decisions agents' queue, fed by each maestro); status files and logs link to the card, they never restate it. The Decide surface shows the total, the oldest age and the count over 48 h at the top. A restated ask is a finding. *Idea: a single source of truth and the "out of sight" cost: an ask in six places is weighed in none.* *Test:* the decisions site count matches the sum of open asks in the status files; zero "Dinis:" lines older than 7 days.

**R10. Make the briefs about change, and say what is not asked.** Each 4 h brief leads with "Decide: N new, oldest X h; Escalate: N" and then what moved and stopped, with every FYI collapsed. A pre-mortem line on each one-way card ("if this goes wrong, the likely reason is …") replaces long background. *Idea: recognition-primed decisions (an expert decides fast when shown the pattern and the risk, not the history) and the pre-mortem.* *Test:* Dinis's own rating after a week (did the brief tell him what he needed, yes or no), and brief length (target under 150 words before the first link).

## 5. For the command centre's record shapes (section 4 of the plan)

Add to `decide`: `default` and `default_at`; `door` (one-way or two-way) instead of only a boolean, with `why_irreversible` (one line); `asked_at` (required); `recommendation` required, not optional; `answer_landed` (time and evidence). Add to every class: `restates` (the id of the card it repeats, which the queue then merges). These are small and make R2, R3, R8 and R9 checkable.
