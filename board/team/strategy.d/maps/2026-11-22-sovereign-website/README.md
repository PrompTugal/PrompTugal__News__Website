## What the map shows

1. **"A website" is a commodity; an owned, governed, data-rich one is not.** Hosted builders sell a site as a commodity, and pay for it with lock-in: none read states an export path (sw-g1, sw-g6), some keep perpetual licences over the content (sw-g7), most charge monthly (sw-g5). Do not compete on "a site". Compete on the four needs they leave unmet: move-out, content terms, where it lives, pay once.
2. **The bottom is already commodity, and free.** Plain files, static hosting and domains cost nothing to us or the owner (sw-g2, sw-g9). The open-source engines give the same output but only to developers (sw-g2, sw-g3). The factory and the agent team turn that commodity into something a non-technical owner can have.
3. **The visitor is the missing user, and the stand proved it.** The owner's journey ends in a purchase; the visitor's journey had no next step. The topic and platform sites, the policy moment and "make it yours" are the bridge: enjoy, try to break it, see it caught, fork it. They are genesis, and they are the B to C to B route in the platform-sites strategy.
4. **The partner multiplies the owner.** Every partner holds many owners; the offer is a draft and unsigned. It turns the maintainers who are today a competitor into the channel.

## Positionings this suggests (for landscape.d's step 4, not a decision)

1. **"Agents build it. You own it, and can move it."** Lead user: the owner. Answers sw-g1, sw-g6, sw-g7.
2. **"Try it on something you love, then try to break it."** Lead user: the visitor. Answers C1.
3. **"Your web person, upgraded."** Lead user: the partner. Maintain, do not replace; start small.
4. **"Pay once."** A price line, not a position on its own (sw-g5).

Which leads is Dinis's call (sherpa.d). My view: 2 leads the traffic and the first step, 1 is what is bought, 3 is how it scales; they are one funnel, not rivals.
