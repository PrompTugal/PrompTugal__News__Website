# 01 What Holacracy is

A short account, in our words, of the parts that matter here. Details are in the sources (08), which we did not all read at first hand.

## Roles, not people
A **role** is a defined piece of work: a **purpose** (why it exists), **domains** (things it alone may control or change) and **accountabilities** (ongoing activities others can expect of it). One person can fill many roles, and one role can be filled by several people, each with a focus. People are not their roles.

## Circles
Roles sit in **circles**. A circle has its own purpose and may contain sub-circles. The largest is the **anchor circle**. A circle is led by a **Circle Lead**, who holds the circle's purpose, assigns people to its roles and sets priorities.

## Core roles in the current constitution (v5.0)
- **Circle Lead**: as above.
- **Circle Rep**: carries the circle's tensions to the broader circle. It is separate from the Lead on purpose, so a problem with the Lead itself still has somewhere to go.
- **Facilitator**: keeps meetings and governance true to the rules.
- **Secretary**: keeps the records and schedules the meetings.

## Tension: the unit of change
A **tension** is a gap between how things are and how they could be. Everything starts from one. A tension about work becomes a next action. A tension about structure (a role missing, two roles on one domain, a rule that does not fit) becomes a **proposal**.

## Two kinds of meeting
- **Tactical**: short, regular: check in, look at numbers, turn tensions into next actions.
- **Governance**: change the roles and policies, by **integrative decision making**: a proposal is adopted unless someone gives a valid objection, one that shows it would make things worse. "Safe enough to try" is the test, not "best possible".

## A constitution and a record
The rules are written in a constitution that the organisation adopts. The current roles, policies and fillers are kept in a **governance record**, one place anyone can read.

## What it is known for
It is also known for friction: critics of its use at Zappos and Medium said the weight of rules and process was the problem, and that people are not built to run like software. That criticism comes up again in 03.
