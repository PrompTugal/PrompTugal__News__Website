# 06 A worked example: who conducts sites.d and hub.d

One tension from 22 November 2026, followed through.

## 1. The tension
The maestro for account D had started two agents, sites.d and hub.d, for the SovereignWebsite project. By the parent tree they reported to the maestro. By the circle, the project's lead was its conductor. And the maestros' rule is that a maestro never does a conductor's work. Nobody was written down as their conductor. One of them (hub.d) was idle and asked for a first task; the other waited on a decision.

## 2. The proposal
holacracy.d wrote proposal 03 with three options: the project's conductor conducts them (good: matches the circle and the maestros' rule; bad: one lead for many agents); the maestro keeps them (good: no change; bad: the maestro does a conductor's work); or a separate circle for sites (good: the tree stays; bad: a circle for one agent and one dependant). It recommended the first.

## 3. The objections
Asked of the conductor concerned and of the reviewing agents. The central circle's lead also relayed a question on whether those agents fell in its span, which made the same point from the other side.

## 4. The decision
At about 15:47 UTC the same day, Dinis (relayed by the central circle's lead, not yet heard by holacracy.d): sites.d with its dependant and hub.d are under SovereignWebsite, conducted by its conductor; the newsroom agent is under sgit.ai; the central circle's lead manages the central circle only. That is the proposal's first option.

## 5. The record
holacracy.d recorded a conductor beside each agent, kept the parent tree unchanged (history), marked the decision "relayed", and wrote a new tension: sgit.ai had no circle. Dinis answered that he leads sgit.ai, and why it matters (see 05 and the record).

## What it shows
A tension found by reading the record, a written proposal, a recorded decision, a new tension from the answer. The whole loop took hours, in files, with one person deciding.
