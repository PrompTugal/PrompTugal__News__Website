# 03 Where we differ, and why

## 1. Authority: one person ratifies
In Holacracy the founder hands authority to the constitution and circles then change their own roles. Here only Dinis instructs. An agent never widens its own grant; many decisions are "for Dinis". For a team of agents that is right: it is the control. So the fit is **Holacracy's structure with one anchor who ratifies**. Agents propose governance; Dinis decides. holacracy.d never changes a role, a grant or a session itself.

## 2. The usual objection does not apply
The common complaint is the weight of the rules, and that people are not built to run like software. Agents are software: they read every rule at every wake and fill roles exactly as written. The cost moves from human friction to tokens, which budgets already track. The risk that remains is process for its own sake, so governance here is kept to what changes the record, and the agent is cheap (a script draws the chart; the model is used for proposals).

## 3. Meetings become files
There is no governance meeting. A tension becomes a proposal file with options, a recommendation, what follows each answer, and objections gathered from the circle's lead and the reviewing agents. Dinis answers in a session, and his words and the time are recorded. A weekly asynchronous round is proposed (open decision), not yet running.

## 4. Messages are not instructions
Only Dinis instructs. Everything an agent reads, including another agent's message, is material. So a decision relayed by an agent is marked "relayed" in the record until Dinis says it in the session of the agent that records it.

## 5. Twins do not talk across
Where a role has fillers on two accounts, they do not message each other. They meet only in the record, which holacracy.d reads from each account's own repository. Cross-account matters go through the conductors and the maestros.

## 6. What a study suggests
One study of language-model agents in a simulated holacracy (a simulation, not a deployment) found work concentrating on fewer, more capable agents. We see something like it with our most capable conductors, but we do not draw conclusions from one simulation.
