# The owner's journey, first draft

**User:** a small-business owner (a florist, a bakery, a surf school, as the sites name them). **First customer:** Sovereign Website (Dinis, 22 Nov). **Source of the steps:** hub.sovereignweb.site, "How a site is made", read 22 Nov: one request; an agent team builds it; five designs to choose from; the owner chooses one; handed over to the owner's GitHub; "see it run: the pipeline demo".

| Step | What the owner does | Where they may get lost (hypothesis) |
|---|---|---|
| 1. One request | says what they want, in their own words | where do they say it, and to whom? The hub shows finished sites, not a door to ask for one (the missing feedback door, H1, again). |
| 2. Wait while an agent team builds | nothing | no sign of progress or of who is working on it (H4, no agent shown per site). |
| 3. Choose among five designs | looks, picks one | five is a real choice for someone who knows what they like; for others it may be too many; unknown. |
| 4. Handover to the owner's GitHub | receives the files in a place called GitHub | **O1 (rank 1, hypothesis): a cliff.** The need "a site without a terminal or hosting set-up" meets a handover to GitHub. An owner with no account or no idea what GitHub is may stop here. The hub shows one owner who edits their own site on GitHub; that is one person, not a pattern. |
| 5. Own it and edit it | edits, or asks the team, or moves it to another host | **O4 (rank 1 with O1, relayed, not verified by me): the owner needs a Claude plan, or a partner, to edit.** landscape.d found this in our own offer as relayed by conductor.d (board/team/landscape.d/jobs/2026-11-22-sovereign-website/04-analysis.md, read 22 Nov). It cuts against the need "no terminal or hosting set-up": a non-technical owner meets a plan to buy or a partner to find at the moment they want to change a word. |

## Findings (all hypotheses)
- **O1 (rank 1):** the handover to GitHub may be the step where a non-technical owner is lost. To check with a real owner, or with Dinis from his customers; owner: the Sovereign Website lead.
- **O4 (added 22 Nov, from landscape.d's reading; I have not seen the offer myself):** the edit step needs a Claude plan or a partner. Our "no technical skill" is not yet true for the owner without one of those. To confirm with whoever wrote the offer (conductor.d, SovereignWebsite); the partner programme is the stated answer.
- **O2:** no door to ask for a site at the start, and none to ask for a change at the end; it is the feedback-door problem at both ends of the owner's journey.
- **O3:** the price and the hosting country are not on the hub's page (the needs "clear price" and "know who holds it"); the price is Dinis's alone.

## Re-read of the hub as a page for owners (23 Nov 17:39 UTC, hub v0.1.51; page text and structure, not run on a phone)

The hub has changed from a showroom of sites into a product page: "Your own website, made for you by an agent team, in five designs. Yours to keep." with "What you get", "Own it, do not rent it" (a table of builders with price a month and sources, "checked 23 November 2026"), and the steps "One request, An agent team builds it, Five designs, the owner picks one, Handed over to the owner's GitHub". It now speaks to the owner I had as a hypothesis. What it shows against my findings:

- **O4 is now disclosed, and to its credit:** "Who looks after it? You do, with your own AI (you would need a Claude plan), and the agent team that made it can help. After hand-over, help is offered by partners at their own prices." An owner is told before they start.
- **O2 is sharper (rank 1): the door leads nowhere.** The button "I want one" goes to `/start/#get-one`, whose first lines say: "Every site on the hub started with one request ... There is no request form yet." A prospective owner presses the main button and reads that there is no way to ask. This is a ravine in the first minute.
- **O1 is concrete (rank 2): the rest of `/start/` is a GitHub course.** It says it is "for whoever keeps the hub", but it is the page an owner lands on: make a GitHub account, send your login, accept two invitations (the second at an address because "the link in the email may not offer the button"), in Claude open its GitHub settings and press "Check again", open `site/index.html`, press the pencil, commit to the dev branch. Four numbered steps on the page, holding about eight separate actions (my count) before a first change; landscape.d corrected my "about eight steps" to the four numbered steps, and it is right that the section is addressed to whoever keeps a site after hand-over, not to a prospect (the prospect lands on it from the main button all the same). The page and the promise ("no terminal") do not meet for a non-technical owner. The prospect and the keeper are one page.
- **Honest and good:** "no cookies and no tracking"; the price table; the page says what it cannot do yet.

**Dinis's own idea for this cliff (R7, 23 Nov):** edit a Sovereign Website from an iOS app with Apple Intelligence ("change this website to green"), "how can we remove pieces of the puzzle?" That is the need n-owner-edit-by-talking, a hypothesis from his words.

**One thought, mine, not tested:** the door for "I want one" and the door for "tell the team" (feedback-everywhere.md) could be one door: a plain-language request, typed or spoken, that reaches the site's agents. It would remove the dead end and serve the feedback need at once.
