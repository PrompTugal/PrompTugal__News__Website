# Do you play the story, or skip to the decision?

**Why this question.** Dinis (22 Nov, in my session): "do you play the story ... I need that to be one of the studio animations here, so I can make a call." It is asked of him because no review can say it: only his use shows it. His earlier word on the brief (12:40 UTC, as relayed by maestro.d): the narrator small on the right with subtitles, the page as the main area, the decision only at the end.

## The journey, as a diagram (for studio.d to animate)

```
Notice a decision  ->  Open its brief  ->  [ story plays ]  ->  decision at the end  ->  answer  ->  receipt
                                 \                                     ^
                                  \__ skip to the decision ____________/
```
Two paths into the same choice. The question is which is the default, and for which decisions.

## Options

| | What happens | Does well | Does badly |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Story first, decision at the end (today) | the brief plays; the decision comes after; skip is available | context is always seen once; the decision is informed; matches his 12:40 word | costs time on the many decisions where "my decision is yes" (his words); the story is the same length for a small yes and for a key decision |
| B. Decision first, story one tap away | opens on the question, the options and the recommendation; "play the story" for the background | fastest for routine yeses; the context stays one tap away | context is easy to skip on a decision that needed it; against his earlier wish for the decision only at the end |
| C. By kind of decision (recommended) | routine decisions open on the decision; his reserved ones (money, law, brand, people, keys, what RiskMandate is) play the story first | fast where yes is safe, careful where it is not; uses the reserved list that J4 found missing | needs each decision marked routine or reserved (an owner's tag, checked by helm.d); a wrong tag misleads |

**Recommendation: C.** It answers both of his words: "a number of times my decision is yes" and "a lot of checks and controls". It depends on the reserved marker (finding J4), which maestro.d has put in the next batch.

## What follows each answer
- **A:** no change to the pages; the length of each story becomes the thing to cut.
- **B:** the briefs reorder: question on top, story below; bulletins.d and maestro.d change the page.
- **C:** each decision gets a kind (routine or reserved); the page opens accordingly; the first few weeks of use show whether he skips the story anyway.

## How we will know
His own use: which stories he plays to the end, which he skips, which decision he answers without playing. A week of that settles it better than any review.

## Caveat
This is my reading of his words and the two pages; it is not a test with him, and the diagram is a sketch, not a design.
