# The stand, and the chasm

**Source:** Dinis, in sherpa.d's session, 22 Nov 2026 (spoken, cleaned lightly). What he saw at the Web Summit stand (9 to 12 November): "tons of people" came; they liked it and were impressed.

## The journey as he told it

| Step | What the visitor does | What they say or feel (his account) |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Walk by | passes the stand, sees the galaxy of our agents | "Ooh, what's that?" |
| 2. Talk | we tell them it is all our agents working | "Ooh, interesting." |
| 3. Why it holds | we say the agents work because of the risk policies, which keep them under control | "Ooh, that's cool." |
| 4. Policies | they ask what the policies are; we explain the difference between the mandate and the grant | "Yeah, that's pretty cool." |
| 5. The offer | "If you have agents, we can help you control them"; we show the full price points | impressed |
| 6. **The chasm** | nothing to do next | **a lot of good leads, but nobody bought directly; no immediate little thing** |

## The finding (rank 1): the chasm
**C1:** between "impressed" and "bought" there was no small first step and nowhere to try the policies. People left with interest and no way to experiment, so the conversation became a lead, not a purchase. Dinis's own words: "We didn't have a good way for them to come out of it and experiment and policy. And that's why these websites are about."

## The need it adds (two users, not one)
- **A visitor who has agents** (or may): try the thing on something they care about, and feel the policies working, before deciding to buy. They do not need to care about policies first; they need to enjoy a site first.
- **Us, as the seller of that experience:** an immediate small step a lead can take straight away, with no call and no purchase.

## The idea Dinis gave for the bridge
A Sovereign Website on a topic the visitor already likes: "do you like football? ... do you want to know about CVEs ... almost any topic that they would want." They come in, experiment, say "ooh, I really like that", and then **experience the policies at hand** on that site. Both the site and the policy moment are the deliverable: this is where "the website is our deployment mechanism" meets the stand's gap.

## What this means for the first customer
There are two users of a Sovereign Website: **its owner** (who gets the site) and **its visitor** (who experiences the policies through it). The stand's chasm is about the visitor. I will map both and show landscape.d, who asked which user leads the positioning; it is Dinis's call which leads.
