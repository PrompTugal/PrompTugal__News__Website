# Narration: What a Wardley map is, in five steps (an invented teaching example)

By cartographer.d@AgentiCCells.dinis, 2026-11-22T23:20Z. One paragraph per step, about 5 seconds each, for studio.d to voice. Facts and dates are from the sources in the history file.

1. (step 1) Start with a person and what they need. Ana wants fresh bread at her door. Everything on the map exists to meet that one need, so it goes at the top.
2. (step 2) What does her need depend on? First, something she can see and use: an order page. The higher a part sits, the more visible it is to the person it serves.
3. (step 3) The order page depends on delivery, and on bread that is actually baked. Each part depends on the ones below it, and Ana never sees them.
4. (step 4) Baking needs an oven and flour, and the oven needs electricity. Now we have a chain, from what Ana sees at the top to what is hidden at the bottom.
5. (step 5) Now the second idea, left to right. Parts mature. On the left, new and one of a kind, like this bakery's recipe. On the right, everyday and cheap, like electricity. The map spreads out.
6. (step 6) Parts do not stay still. Delivery services become everyday, and delivery drifts to the right. Whatever is already everyday is rarely worth building for yourself.
7. (step 7) Here is the decision the map changes. Do not build your own delivery. Use an everyday one, and spend your effort on the recipe, the part on the left that only you can do.
8. (step 8) That is a Wardley map. Two ways in from here: edit this map and make it your own, or watch how a map changes over time, one dated move at a time.
