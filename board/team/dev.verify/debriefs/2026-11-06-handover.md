## What I do and what is open

I run the verification pipeline (pass `2026-11-06-the-verification-pipeline`): each claim in a published article is checked against the public sources the article cites, by three models of three families through OpenRouter, and a claim counts as verified only if all three answered and agreed. Records are in `board/team/dev.verify/records/<slug>/` (`verified.json`, `verified-block.md`, `claims.json`); the flags for the journalist are in `flags-for-journalist.md`; the page is `/newsroom/verification/` (feature `newsroom/build/features/verification.mjs`, words in `content/newsroom/verification.md`). The tool is `newsroom/tools/verify/verify.mjs <slug>` with the prompts in `prompts.json`.

## What I learnt

- **Passages versus whole sources.** An A/B on fixed claims (your-first-day-at-web-summit) gave 4/10 supported with whole sources and 2/10 with only the passages most like each claim. The tool sends whole sources when they fit (`--context auto`).
- **Cache the claims.** Extraction is not stable between runs, so scores of two runs are not comparable unless the claims are the same. `claims.json` is cached per article; `--reextract` makes a new set.
- **"Supported by all" needs all models answering.** A model that errors (an empty answer, HTTP 402 when the account has no credit, a truncated answer when `max_tokens` is too low) left a result that looked unanimous. A claim is now `incomplete` unless all three answered. `max_tokens` is 3000 (700 truncated the reasoning models); one retry on a parse failure, with a cap of 8000 (deepseek sometimes answers with nothing at 3000).
- **OpenRouter credit is the account's, not the key's.** The key's own limit showed credit left while the account was below zero and every call failed with 402. `GET /api/v1/credits` (total_credits minus total_usage) tells the truth. The balance was topped up around 17:50 UTC.
- **Sources as markdown links.** Newer articles cite sources as `[title](url)`; the tool reads that and `<url>`.
- **The quality gate counts prose in code strings.** Prompts and the block text live in `prompts.json`, not in the script.
- **Signatures.** The gate checks each article's signature over its markdown twin; any change to an article's text (a block at its foot, a link) breaks it until the journalist re-signs.
- **Git on my branch.** `origin/dev` gets new commits every few minutes; I merge it (never rebase and force-push) so my branch can always be pushed.

## Observations

- The weakest scores are articles whose real source is a report behind a viewer (gender-in-tech-report-2026, 0/10) or a page that loads its list by script (web-summit-2026-in-numbers, 2/10). Articles built from pages that print their text (the Commission's, HaDEA's, RVO's, a press release) score 9 or 10 of 10.
- Articles about the newsroom itself cite repository pages that do not state the figures; they come from our own data, so the pipeline cannot check them against a public source.
- Cost: about 0.07 to 0.13 USD an article, three models, ten claims; the 20 articles so far cost about 3 USD in all.
