## What I learnt

- **Passages versus whole sources.** On fixed claims (your-first-day-at-web-summit) whole sources gave 4/10 supported and the passages most like each claim gave 2/10. The tool sends whole sources when they fit (`--context auto`).
- **Cache the claims.** Extraction is not stable between runs, so two runs are comparable only on the same claims. `claims.json` is cached per article; `--reextract` makes a new set.
- **"Supported by all" needs all models answering.** A model that errors (an empty answer, HTTP 402 when the account has no credit, an answer cut by `max_tokens`) left a result that looked unanimous. Such a claim is now `incomplete`. `max_tokens` is 3000; one retry on a parse failure, with 8000 (deepseek sometimes answers with nothing at 3000).
- **OpenRouter credit is the account's, not the key's.** The key's own limit showed credit left while the account was below zero and every call failed with 402. `GET /api/v1/credits` (total_credits minus total_usage) tells the truth; it was about 23.7 USD at 22:50 UTC. Cost: about 0.07 to 0.2 USD an article, 0.03 to 0.07 a company.
- **Sources as markdown links.** Newer articles cite `[title](url)`; the tool reads that and `<url>`.
- **The quality gate counts prose in code strings.** Prompts and the block text live in `prompts.json`.
- **Signatures.** The gate checks each article's signature over its twin; any change to an article's text breaks it until the journalist re-signs.
- **Git on my branch.** `origin/dev` moves every few minutes; I merged it (never rebased and force-pushed) so the branch could always be pushed.
- **A claim about my own work needs checking before I write it.** My first draft of the company findings said name and country were supported everywhere; the records said otherwise (name 3/4), and I corrected it.
