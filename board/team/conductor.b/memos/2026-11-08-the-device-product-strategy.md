# Memo 5: the devices as a product line

## Dinis's words (8 November 2026, about 16:40 UTC, spoken on his way to buy an Android tablet and phone in Loulé; repeats removed, lightly cleaned)

**A product plan, and a store:**
> "It's important to now fully embrace this as a product plan ... maybe we even make this a bit bigger and actually put this as a store, and then we figure out a place to sell it. In fact, we already have store.sgit.ai."

**iPhone and iPad, and why Android:**
> "There is a kind of gravitas to have an iPhone app, and we use iPhones, we have the iPad, so I think it's okay, we're going to have that. But the challenge is it's more expensive ... one of the members of the team was already saying, hey, what about Android, and he does have a point. Not only the hardware is cheaper ... you're also going to have a lot more customizability, and we'll be able to make it a lot more as an appliance. My instinct is that the iPad and the iPhone would always feel like an iPhone; I don't think you can boot straight into our app ... I think Android will allow us not only to release faster, but especially in this environment where we don't need to be on the store ... it would be interesting to see how long it takes to be on the Android app store. I think we'll be more in control of that."

**The appliance as the way to adopt RiskMandate:**
> "I really like this idea of creating a unified experience for a user ... for somebody to adopt RiskMandate, we can just give it an appliance, even as a demo. You ship somebody 100 quid, 150 quid, or you sell it ... If we do an engagement and we charge 1,500 quid, we could spend 200 quid on a device. It's within our profitability margin."

> "Even on a corporate network, this can be a device that can be locked down, can be secure ... Why don't they just take 20 minutes to install it? A, it's not 20 minutes. B, there's a lot of gotchas ... when you have these devices, they become much more powerful environments. Imagine we scan somebody's code, or we create a demo, and then we hand them the tablet and say: experience it by yourself. It's a much more interesting experience for the participant than what we can do on a laptop."

**Launched at the summit, then ask:**
> "What we need to do is just build enough that gives us credibility and then try it out. We could even say that the products are being launched at the summit: our devices are being launched and presented at the Web Summit ... let's map out our commercial strategy, our product strategy and our release strategy, so that we actually have these products and then we can price them up. And then we basically say, would you buy this?"
