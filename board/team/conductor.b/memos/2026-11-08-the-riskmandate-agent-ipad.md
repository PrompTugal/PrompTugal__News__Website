# Memo 2: the RiskMandate agent iPad

## Dinis's words (8 November 2026, about 13:35 UTC, spoken, lightly cleaned)

> "We now actually just bought a couple of iPads because, A, the team didn't have them, and B, actually we want to give the iPad now to the agent. So we're going to have a couple of iPads that are going to be owned and mapped. The agent is basically going to create an Apple ID account for them, which actually makes a lot of things easier, and again is a good example of isolation, and a good example of us packaging."

> "What I want us to map out, and I want to create a product: I want to sell this iPad as a RiskMandate product. A product where we take into account the cost of the iPad. Think about it: for the 1,500 quid or 2 grand, we can slap an iPad in there."

> "Can you start mapping the business proposition? We need to map the value, what we can sell, and then create a product that somebody can buy, so we can actually sell them at the summit. The advantage is that everything is then integrated, and people just buy a piece of kit that we can sell them, and then we'll take it from there."

## What the mapping has to answer

1. **The proposition, in one line**: what a buyer gets (an iPad that is an agent's own device: its own Apple account, its own apps, its own vault, its own policy) and the problem it solves (agents you can see, isolate and keep within budget: memo 1).
2. **What is in the box**: the iPad; the set-up (the agent's account, the PrompTugal app or its successor, the vault, the Agent Behaviour Policy, the budget view); what runs on it on day one; what support follows.
3. **The value**: why isolation on a separate device matters (the agent's identity, keys and spend kept apart from a person's); the cost control story of memo 1 shown on the device itself; the before, during and after.
4. **Who buys it at the summit**: which of the attendee personas (founder, CISO, investor, a company deploying agents), and what they do with it on Monday.
5. **The offer**: price bands around Dinis's "1,500 quid or 2 grand" (the iPad's cost, the set-up, the policy, a period of service); what is a one-off and what recurs.
6. **How it is bought at the summit**: the order, the payment, delivery or hand-over, after-sales.
7. **What has to be true first** (Dinis, 13:55: the device's Apple account is the agent's own Google Workspace email account, which a person holds; a dedicated account on a dedicated iPad is the security point; see memo 3) (for Dinis, before anything is sold): Apple's terms for a device and an account set up for an agent (an organisation's devices usually use Apple Business Manager and Managed Apple Accounts, rather than a personal Apple ID: to check at Apple's own pages); reselling hardware (warranty, VAT, returns, the legal entity that sells, which is Dinis's call); what RiskMandate may claim (only what riskmandate.ai and the team's site say).
