## Dinis's words (8 November 2026, about 13:20 UTC, spoken, lightly cleaned)

> "It's kind of a positioning for us, but it also is kind of very aligned with Indi, which is going to drive, but I think a lot of us are going to do."

> "When we talk to the possible individuals, one of the things we need to figure out is what's in their mind. The thing about RiskMandate traditionally, and I think we already found this, is that it's very easy for somebody to say, yes, I'm worried about my agentic control, I'm worried about my agents. But there's a world of difference between that and somebody who even has a mandate, who even has the ability to actually do something about it. And it becomes a very loaded question. Where I think the budget, and the idea to control the budget, even them personally, they can do something about it."

> "When somebody comes to us: can you control the costs of your agents? Do you have the ability to control the costs of what your agents are doing? I think most people would say no. And more importantly, they can actually do something about it, which I think is very relevant. Which is why I think we need to focus on the costs: not to pivot, but to have a focus on the budget, on what we can do to show the agent behaviour policies aligned with budget control."

> "Normally we would have to come up with some esoteric scenario around this demo. But the beauty of this now is that we now have the perfect scenario, which is actually how these all things started: we're going to show how we can control the budget and we can map the cost of everything we're doing, across the agentic workflows that we have here and the multiple teams that we have. Not only can we show that we're doing all this stuff, we can actually show the cost, and also we can show before, during, and after, which is exactly the moat."

> "Let's give this mandate to Indi ... so that we map the accountant, and we map the whole thing that should be happening ... and then he can own this vertical. Indi can focus on social media, but also can have the agents that are thinking about this. In the past I called this agent the ambassador ... the one that connects the dots (my SG/Send role definitions have that job description). Then he can focus on getting the messaging, the demos, the concept, mapping the user journey for somebody to start controlling the budget."

> "This is the whole point of the agent behaviour policies: if the behaviour is cost control, we can show these really cool workflows, across multiple agents, multiple teams, multiple flows, which is ridiculously powerful. Taking into account the iPad that we're now going to use for agents, and the accounts that we're going to be using for this, I think is a really powerful flow. Let's map the strategy, connect the dots, brief these agents, and connect the view there."

## What Dinis hopes Indi's side will consider owning

1. **The budget vertical**: the positioning (agent behaviour policies as cost control), the messaging, the demos, the concept.
2. **The user journey** for someone who starts controlling their agents' budget: the first question ("can you control what your agents cost?"), what they see, what they do next.
3. **An ambassador agent** that connects the dots across the teams (Dinis's earlier role of that name in the SG/Send project's role definitions is the job description to start from), alongside Indi's social media work.
4. **The demo across teams**: our own agentic workflows as the live case, on the iPad, with the accounts we run.
