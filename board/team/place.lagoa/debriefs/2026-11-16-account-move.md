## 4. Lessons learned
- **What worked:** verify every item on its own page before adding it; the council's RSS feeds are a cheap, stable check; searching in Portuguese plus the parish names (Carvoeiro, Ferragudo, Porches, Estômbar, Parchal) found the parish-level AI item; Algarve Evolution's own events pages are allowed (meetup.com is not) and gave the 2026 hackathon; a company's own site is a good source for what it sells.
- **What failed, and why:** postal.pt (bot check) and sulinformacao.pt (403) cannot be read, so their Lagoa coverage is missing; WebFetch cannot read compressed PDFs (save and use `pdftotext`); some pages time out or return empty (the MEO smart city page, The Portugal News Nobel piece), so I left those claims out.
- **Traps:**
  - Search results mix in Lagoa in the Azores (São Miguel): AI courses at its secondary school are not ours.
  - A search summary is not a source: SPIC was said to be Lagoa-based but RTP says Loulé and the Jornal do Algarve page returned 403, so it stayed out.
  - Stories that sound smart-city but never mention AI fail the test: the City as a Platform prize, the Ferragudo AR portal, the council WhatsApp channel, the Carvoeiro Clube Group's EUR 100 million plan.
  - Page dates: some fetched pages show the viewing date in the header, not the publication date, so check the byline.
  - `release.sh` needs branch `dev`, not a detached HEAD, and loses races when many agents push.
  - The gate prints many WARN lines from others' work: only `OK` matters.
  - Voice text must not add claims: when the facts change, change the voice lines too.
  - Several hooks print "file changed on disk" after your own commit: it is not another agent.
- **Differently:** go for the council feeds, the company-register route and the Algarve Evolution pages first, and ask Dinis early about the blocked outlets; I decided to go `live` late, at 06:50 UTC, which the brief allowed.
