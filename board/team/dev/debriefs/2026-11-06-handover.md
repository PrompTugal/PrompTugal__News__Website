## What I learnt

- **Bring workers' work as one squash commit per branch**, never a merge: `release.sh` rebases, and merges do not survive it. The script that does it is described below; take only the files the worker's own commits touched (`git log --no-merges --name-only origin/dev..origin/work/<b>`), because a worker's branch can carry other agents' stale files (dev.data's branch carried 112 deleted outreach drafts all day).
- **A held branch may be my fault, not the worker's.** dev.graphs' round 2 "invalid JSON" was conflict markers from my own squash against its round 1. Where only that worker's reviews ever touched a conflicted file, take the worker's version.
- **Two agents can build the same thing at the same wake** (the newsletter: dev.pages@ and I, 16:08 to 16:14). Look at the workers' branches before starting a feature; when it happens, combine the best of both and say so on both lanes.
- **The quality ratchet applies to me too:** a new code file may hold at most 1024 bytes of prose and 4 HTML tags. Markup goes in a component under `assets/<name>/` (C's is a template, `assets/top/top.tmpl`, filled by `lib/top.mjs`); words go in `data/site/*.json` with a schema.
- **The Pages deploy is cancelled when another queues behind it** (about 5 s after creation; 15:00 to 15:28 nothing deployed). It recovers when a dispatched run lands; `release.sh` says so after 6 minutes. Not mine to fix (devops@), but check the live version after every release.
- **Check a phone at 390 px for width** after any change to the top or the page: a fixed bar stretches to the widest content, so one long unbreakable line (the agent block's URL) moved the tab bar off the screen.
- **Verify a relayed request against Dinis's own words** in `board/guidance/` before acting (Q1 and C both traced to his words).
