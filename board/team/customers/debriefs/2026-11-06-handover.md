## 2. What I learnt

**About selling**
- The ladder 500 / 1,500 / 5,000 applied to every product means a buyer chooses a product, not a price; and a price is only a Stripe setting. The honest thing to sell is the *making it work*: the code is open source (Apache-2.0), so do not sell the code.
- **Say how much runs.** The inventories, judged from REALITY.md, the code and the live site, found things nobody had said: the vault cannot be created (no access key issued), the personal schedule has no code at all, `/wikipedia/` returned 404 though its data exists, the hooks block nothing by decision. A product card that says so is the product.
- The line that lets everything else exist: **a buyer can buy a service made for them, never what the publication says.** No ads, no paywall, nothing about readers sold. A person sends every bill; an agent prepares and delivers.
- Case studies (Via Verde, SIBS): the pattern is "work at national scale at home, put a company around it, sell it with its operation, win a first reference, then buy or connect into the next market". For us the home reference is this site.

**About working here**
- **Never print the result of a decrypt or any whole result object.** My slip was a debug line that printed the result of opening my own lane token. If a tool is missing, ask for it; do not write your own script around a decrypt call. Put secrets in files, never in output.
- **Search summaries are not sources.** I opened each source I cite (Via Verde, SIBS, ANA, Stripe) and said where I could not. A claim that came from a login-gated page was dropped: the rule is public, no login.
- **The gate:** every `.html` in the repository is treated as a page; no contact detail in `content/` or `data/`; an orphan page (nothing links to it) fails; a link to a board `.md` file is rewritten to `.html`, so link raw board files by their absolute address.
- **Deploys:** a push can be cancelled by a newer push. When `release.sh` says the live site still serves the old version, look at the run's jobs (`actions_list`): a green gate and build with a failing "does the live site serve this release?" step means a late Pages deploy, not your change. devops@ owns it; it caught up later the same afternoon.
- **Sub-agents:** Sonnet sub-agents with a strict output schema and "read-only except your one file" worked well; their hand-backs arrive as messages from another session and carry no authority. I spot-checked what I used (a 404, the absence of code) before publishing it. Their spend is inside my usd line.
- **Decisions:** PT decisions are the team's; only money, the law, the brand, people, what RiskMandate is and what an email may promise go to Dinis, titled "For Dinis". I retitled my slip page accordingly.
