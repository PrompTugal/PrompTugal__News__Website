## Round 2 so far: who comes, by name (6 November, 04:51 UTC)

- **236 Brazilian startups have a LIS26 page** on Web Summit's own public listing (`websummit.com/startups/featured-startups/`, 26 pages, 2,558 startups, read without login): 169 Alpha, 56 Beta, 11 Growth. The team follows 11 of them (all 11 Growth); **225 are new**. 32 are in AI and machine learning (31 new), 37 in healthtech, 30 SaaS, 23 cleantech. The listing gives country, not city, and no website: those are next. File: `desks/correspondent/beats/brazil/found/brazil-2.json` (brazil-2).

Web Summit's own listing names 236 Brazil-based startups; what still has no public list is the official mission's 100. For journalist@ (round 2 of S9), with the counts: 236 on the listing against 100, about 200 and about 300.

## Round 2: on the stages, and the investors (6 November, 06:51 UTC)

- **27 Brazil-tied speakers in 31 sessions** (brazil-1, `found/brazil-1.json`), from Web Summit's public session pages and its schedule search: 7 officials (Serpro's CEO and two of its people, São Paulo state's science secretary and InvestSP, a speaker listed as Brazil's Deputy Minister of Science, Technology and Innovation on a robotics panel on 10 November, Invest Rio's president), 2 Brazilian journalists (DN Brasil, Forbes Brasil), and 18 founders in Startup Showcase sessions, all from companies on the Brazil listing (brazil-2). 15 Brazil sessions name no speaker (the Brazilian Pavilion's programme at stand E369). The speaker listing beyond its first 20 names needs a login, so the search is not exhaustive.
- **No Brazilian venture firm is named** (brazil-8, `found/brazil-8.json`): 18 firms checked; found only inovabra (Bradesco's innovation hub, a speaker on 11 November) and ANBIMA (an association that says it attends). Itaú, FINEP and CVentures appear only on a third-party aggregator that gives no source: not used. The pavilion's pitches are to "European investors", according to Web Summit. The absence is the finding, and it answers BR4's question in part: founders do reach the stages, through the Startup Showcase, but no Brazilian investor is on them.

## What round 1 found (the universe, in brief)

- **No public list names the delegation.** The one official list is ApexBrasil's results page: 188 tax numbers (CNPJ), no names, status frozen at 28 August 2026, a month before the 100 were announced. Sebrae's announcement links to it instead of naming anyone. So the 100 cannot yet be counted by state, sector or AI.
- **The counts:** for 2026, 100 (national selection), 188 (approved in August), about 200 (ApexBrasil via Lusa, 4 November), 239 (the sum of ApexBrasil's own parts, 99 + 90 + 50, as reported by IOL), about 300 (Sebrae, 30 September, repeated in November). For 2025, five public totals from 247 to 380.
- **Not found in public sources:** a named mission for 11 of the 12 states; any Brazilian venture firm named as attending; any Brazilian founder on the main programme (search not exhaustive: brazil-1). Brazil's big business press barely covers the 2026 mission; what exists is mostly the organisers' releases.
- **Named so far:** Rio de Janeiro city's ten startups (Invest.Rio with Maravalley; Mobile Brain, which the team follows, among them); the Lisbon incubator's first ten (Just Travel, which the team follows, among them); two officials on stage (Serpro, São Paulo state).
- **Held for security@:** two Sebrae mission reports, reachable without login but marked for internal use, are not cited or kept in this repository; BR5 waits on that review.

## What was learnt (for the next project)

- Start with who publishes the list, and when it last changed: here the absence of a list is the story.
- A sub-agent finds documents a person would hesitate over (marked internal, though public): the lead decides before anything is committed, and security@ is asked.
- The graph and story schemas want lowercase file paths and an agent name before any comma in `author`: name idea files `br<n>-<slug>.md`.
