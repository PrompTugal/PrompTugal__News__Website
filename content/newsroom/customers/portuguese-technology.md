**A case study for the customers section, asked for by Dinis Cruz on 6 November 2026.** The question: what Portuguese technology has been sold abroad or could be, how, and what do we learn about selling ours? Every fact below has its source and the date it was read (6 November 2026). Where a source was a search summary I did not open, or I found nothing, it says so.

## In short {#short}

- **Via Verde** sold its tolling back office and its running of a motorway toll to the Netherlands. The technology (from a Brisa group company, A-to-Be) was proven at home first, and it was sold **together with the running of it**, in a contract of two years to build and five to operate.
- **SIBS**, which runs Multibanco and MB WAY, goes abroad in two ways: by **buying local operators** (a Polish ATM and payments company, in a country where it has worked since 2008) and by **connecting its network to other countries' schemes** (the first MB WAY payments with Spain's Bizum and Italy's Bancomat, 13 November 2024).
- **ANA**, the airports company, earns about a quarter of its revenue from **commercial space and services** (shops, food, advertising, parking, car rental): EUR 366 million in 2025. That is a different model from the other two. It sells access to a place full of people, not a technology. I found no evidence that ANA sells technology.
- **None of the three is in our data for the summit** (read 6 November 2026). The nearest Portuguese companies that are: Feedzai and MEO, each with a confirmed speaker.
- **The pattern:** work at national scale at home, put a company around the technology, sell the technology **with its operation**, win a first reference abroad, then buy or connect your way into the next market. For us, the home reference is this site, and the thing to sell is the making-it-work, not the code. See [what we can package](../products/index.html).

## The three cases {#cases}

| | What works at home | What was sold abroad | How |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Via Verde** (Brisa group) | the electronic toll service | an electronic toll operation on the A24 motorway near Rotterdam; later, per-kilometre tolls for heavy vehicles across almost all Dutch motorways | a tender won in December 2022 (EUR 16 million: two years to implement, five to operate, two optional extensions of two years each) using A-to-Be's "MoveBeyond Tolling BackOffice", described as fully developed in Portugal; the toll began on 7 December 2024; a consortium led by a Brisa group company with Ascendi O&M and Yunex Traffic runs the heavy-vehicle toll, in force since 1 July 2026 |
| **SIBS** (Multibanco, MB WAY) | the national ATM and payments network, and the MB WAY mobile payment | processing and ATM services in Poland, and instant payments between MB WAY and Bizum (Spain) and Bancomat (Italy) | in Poland since 2008; in September 2025 it announced buying ITCARD, a Polish payments outsourcing firm founded in 2010 that runs the Planet Cash ATM network (about 5,500 ATMs in Poland; SIBS would manage more than 20,000 ATMs across its operations) and Planet Pay terminals; the cross-border payments were announced on 13 November 2024 |
| **ANA** (airports) | its airports' shops, food, advertising, parking and car rental | not a technology: a record EUR 1.4 billion of revenue in 2025 (up 9%), of which EUR 366 million (up 15%) was non-aviation | from the airports' own commercial space; aviation was 73.9% of revenue, so non-aviation about 26% |

### Via Verde {#via-verde}

- Source for the tender and the technology: [Observador, 23 December 2022](https://observador.pt/2022/12/23/via-verde-ganha-concurso-de-16-milhoes-de-euros-nos-paises-baixos/): Via Verde won a EUR 16 million tender to run electronic toll collection on the A24 near Rotterdam; the bid used A-to-Be's Portuguese technology, "totalmente desenvolvida em Portugal"; two years of implementation, five of operation, two optional two-year extensions. The article does not say who awarded it.
- Source for the start and what A-to-Be is: [Forbes Portugal, 6 December 2024](https://www.forbespt.com/?p=144819): Via Verde, through its Dutch subsidiary, began managing the A24 toll on 7 December; A-to-Be is "the technology company for infrastructure of the Brisa Group", and developed the hardware and software; the company had previously entered other markets through its parent.
- Source for the heavy-vehicle toll: [ECO, citing Lusa, 8 July 2026](https://eco.sapo.pt/2026/07/08/via-verde-inicia-cobranca-de-portagens-de-pesados-por-quilometro-nos-paises-baixos/): since 1 July 2026, per-kilometre tolls for vehicles over 3.5 tonnes on almost the whole Dutch motorway network, by GPS/GNSS, with no barriers; the contract went to the NedLinq consortium, led by a Brisa group company. **The article does not say that A-to-Be built this system, nor give its value.**

(The headline says 20,000 ATMs in Poland; the body gives that figure as the total across SIBS's operations, and about 5,500 for Poland.)

## What the pattern says {#pattern}

1. **The home market is the reference.** Via Verde's technology ran a national toll before it was bid abroad; SIBS's network is the one the country uses. A buyer abroad is buying "it works, at that scale, for years".
2. **A company is put around the technology.** A-to-Be is, in Forbes Portugal's words, the Brisa group's technology company for infrastructure; SIBS has a Polish business selling processing and ATM services. The technology is sold by a team whose job is to sell and run it.
3. **The technology is sold with its operation.** The Dutch contract is two years to build and five to run. A buyer does not want a licence; they want the toll to work.
4. **Connect or buy to get into the next market.** SIBS buys local operators and links to other countries' payment schemes, rather than asking a country to adopt its network.
5. **Selling the place is a separate business.** ANA's commercial revenue comes from the space and the footfall. It needs no technology and no export, and it only works where the people already are.
