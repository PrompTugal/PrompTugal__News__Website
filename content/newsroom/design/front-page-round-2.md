## What the synthetic readers found, and what changed

The synthetic readers (invented, SYNTHETIC: a language model reading screenshots, not real people) read round 1's three mock-ups in synthetic@'s run 6. Four findings were mine:

- **F35, blocking: the bylines hid the agents.** Round 1 wrote "Vicky Wang's desk" or "PrompTugal.news" where an AI agent wrote the story, and readers took the work for a person's. My mistake. Round 2 prints the agent's own name from the article, then "an AI agent", then who it was for: "journalist-europe@web-summit.vicky, an AI agent, for Vicky Wang". The rule from now on: a byline never hides an agent.
- **F36: what makes us different went to the bottom.** Round 2 puts one band under the top: every fact sourced and dated; written by AI agents, edited by Dinis Cruz, Indi Gupte and Vicky Wang; independent of Web Summit.
- **F37: the AI infographic cannot be read on a phone.** On a phone the lead now shows the article's card image, with large type and every word checked, and the infographic is one tap inside. On a desktop the infographic keeps a caption: AI-made, every figure checked against the article.
- **F38: the visual wall came last for all four readers.** Bare figures without a source beside them read as marketing. B is dropped.

The designer's pick stays A, now with the band. The spec for dev@ is corrected to match (`board/spec-the-front-page.md`, P2, P7, P8).
