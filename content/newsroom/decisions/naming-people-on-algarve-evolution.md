## The question

Should the Algarve Evolution page name the people who lead it, and if so, when? Today it names nobody: it says "its executive director" and describes the organisation. The meeting on Saturday is the natural place to ask them.

## What we know, in roles only

The researcher's round 6 (`desks/researcher/algarve-evolution-round-1.md`, read 6 November 2026) found three public professional roles, each in dated press sources:

- The **president**, elected for 2024 to 2027 and described as the chief executive of a strategy consultancy ([postal.pt, 11 July 2024](https://postal.pt/economia/joaquim-nascimento-e-o-novo-presidente-da-algarve-evolution/)). Whether this is still so in 2026 is not confirmed, and the organisation's own site lists no leadership.
- The **outgoing president**, thanked by the association in the same article.
- The **executive director**, named in that role by two independent sources: [postal.pt, 6 June 2024](https://postal.pt/algarve/impact-algarve-juntou-principais-intervenientes-locais-para-debaterem-o-futuro-da-regiao/) and [Portugal Startup News, 1 November 2026](https://portugalstartupnews.com/2026/11/01/algarve-evolution-aims-to-support-500-companies-and-help-create-150-startups-by-2032-in-push-beyond-tourism/).

None of these people has been asked. No photo under a free licence has been found, and none is used.

## The options

### A. Roles only (what is live now)

The page keeps saying "executive director" and "president" without names.

- **Does well:** nothing about a person is published without their say; nothing to correct; safe for the meeting.
- **Does badly:** the page reads as less of a portrait of the organisation than it could be; the person across the table sees we know the role and chose not to say who.
- **Work:** none.

### B. Name them after each says yes (recommended)

Dinis asks at the meeting. Each person who agrees is named on the page with their public professional role and the dated source; the page says "named with their agreement". Anyone who declines stays as a role.

- **Does well:** respects the rule that a person is listed only when what we say is public and professional and, where it is ours to ask, with their yes; turns the meeting into the first correction round; a photo can follow, under a free licence or one they give.
- **Does badly:** waits for the meeting and for each answer; the page will be the same until then.
- **Work:** `dev.portugal@` adds the section the same day; `researcher@` checks a free-licence photo once names are agreed; `security@` reviews.

### C. Name them now, from the press

The page names the president and the executive director with their roles and sources today.

- **Does well:** the page is complete when Dinis shows it; every fact is already public and sourced.
- **Does badly:** names people who have not been asked, on a page about their own organisation, before a meeting with one of them; the president's role in 2026 is unconfirmed, so it risks a wrong or stale claim; it breaks the order the researcher and the CLAUDE.md rules set.
- **Work:** `dev.portugal@` adds the section; `security@` reviews.

## Recommendation

**B.** It costs one conversation and turns the page's weakest section into its most credible one. C is allowed by the sources but not by the courtesy we owe the people named.

## What follows each answer

- **A:** nothing changes; the question comes back once the organisation has agreed to be named.
- **B:** after the meeting, tell the conductor who agreed and for what role; `dev.portugal@` publishes at its next wake.
- **C:** `dev.portugal@` publishes at its next wake, with the dated source on each line.
