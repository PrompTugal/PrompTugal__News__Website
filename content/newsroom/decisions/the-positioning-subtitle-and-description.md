## The question {#question}

On 15 November (23:59 UTC) you asked for the positioning to move from the Summit to **Portugal and local news made by an agentic newsroom**, with sherpa@ leading the wording. The name is yours and the brand is yours, so this is a For Dinis page. What is live today (read on 16 November on a phone at 390 px and a desktop):

- the app is still "PrompTugal: Lisbon Summit" on TestFlight;
- the site's top bar says "Portugal · town by town · Independent", and the front page says "People and AI agents, reporting together";
- `/contribute/` still opens "starting with Web Summit Lisbon, 10 to 12 November 2026".

Three different promises on one visit. A cold reader cannot tell which is the product.

## What the line has to do {#test}

A stranger who has never heard of us must know, in the first phone screen: **what it is** (news), **where** (Portugal, then their town), **why trust it** (sourced) and, for the people we want as partners, **that it is not a replacement for them**. Your words on 16 November: not about replacing journalism, about scaling journalism and making journalism pay. The App Store limit is 30 characters.

## The options, in full {#options}

The name stays **PrompTugal.news** in every option.

### A. "Local news, town by town" (24 characters)
- **Does well:** says what and how it is organised; a reader in Loulé reads it as theirs; plain Portuguese translates it: "Notícias locais, vila a vila".
- **Does badly:** says nothing about trust, nothing about AI. Any local paper could print it.
- **Reader view (sherpa@'s reading, not yet tested on the personas):** the local reader understands at once. A journalist or a local editor reads "another local news site", a competitor.

### B. "Local news, every fact sourced" (30 characters)
- **Does well:** the promise is checkable: every fact has a link and the date it was read, which is true of the place pages today. It is the line a journalist respects.
- **Does badly:** "local" is the only place word, so Portugal is lost; exactly at the limit, so any edit breaks it.
- **Reader view (sherpa@'s reading, not yet tested on the personas):** strongest for the sceptical reader (journalist, editor, investor). Weaker for a founder who wants to know it is about technology.

### C. "Local AI news, town by town" (27 characters)
- **Does well:** keeps the technology and AI beat (the PrompTugal test: about AI within two or three steps) and the places. Matches the home page's own headline for the Algarve, "an AI agent for every Algarve town".
- **Does badly:** "AI news" reads as news *made by* AI to some readers and *about* AI to others; to a local editor it can sound like the threat. It is ambiguous in exactly the place the values page has to be clear.
- **Reader view (sherpa@'s reading, not yet tested on the personas):** founder and investor: clear. Journalist and local editor: reads as replacement.

### D. "Local news, made in the open" (28 characters)
- **Does well:** the openness is what is unique (you can watch the agents, anyone can correct a fact), and it is partner-friendly: nothing is hidden from the people it quotes.
- **Does badly:** abstract; "in the open" means little before you have seen an agent's file.
- **Reader view (sherpa@'s reading, not yet tested on the personas):** good for the contributor and the investor, thin for the casual local reader.

### E. "Portugal, town by town" (22 characters)
- **Does well:** short, true, the geography is the product, and it already sits in the site's top bar and its page title.
- **Does badly:** no word for news, so in a store list it could be a travel or tourism app.
- **Reader view (sherpa@'s reading, not yet tested on the personas):** works on the site, where the page itself says news; risky as a store line.

## The two-line description (the same for every option) {#description}

> One AI agent for each Algarve town, and for Portugal's district capitals, reads the local press and public records and keeps a signed file of what it found, every fact linked to its source and the date it was read.
> We send readers, and in time money, to the local media who did the reporting: we scale journalism, we do not replace it. Anyone can correct a fact.

(Two sentences, 66 words; if the store wants shorter, drop "and in time money".)

The site's tagline would then replace "People and AI agents, reporting together" with the chosen subtitle on the front page, and `/contribute/` would open with "Write for your town".

## sherpa@'s recommendation {#recommendation}

**B for the store, A as the tagline under the logo on the site**, with the description above.

- The store line is read once, cold, by a stranger who must decide to trust it: B is the only option that makes a promise they can check, and trust is what separates us from a feed of summaries.
- On the site the reader is already inside a place and has the page around the line, so the shorter, warmer A works and keeps "local" without the legal weight of "every fact".
- C is the one to avoid in the first screen: it is the most accurate about the beat and the most likely to read as the replacement your 16 November words rule out. "AI" belongs in the description and in the places, not the subtitle.
- Dissent to expect: correspondent@ may want the beat (technology and AI) in the line; the answer is the description's first sentence.

## What happens after each answer {#after}

- **You choose a letter:** sherpa@ sends the line and the description to conductor.a.ios@ for App Store Connect; designer@ and dev@ change the tagline and the page title; journalist@ rewrites the `/contribute/` opening and checks the new line is on `/values/`.
- **You choose "none of these":** say what the line must carry; sherpa@ and synthetic@ rewrite and test on a journalist, a local editor and a local reader before it comes back.
- **A/B on the site:** if you prefer, A and B run as two arms on the front page and the winner goes to the store (the PT-decision rule for unsure cases, 6 November).
