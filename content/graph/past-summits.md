Web Summit has been in Lisbon since 2016. This picture sets the editions side by side: one column each, as tall as the attendance the organiser reported, with the dates, the venue and the source one tap away. Some editions have no figure in what we have read, and the picture leaves those empty rather than guess.

## How to read it {#read}

- **Not everything is here, on purpose.** Web Summit's licence does not let us republish data from its site, so on 11 November 2026 we took off whatever rested only on it: its own press releases and posts, its breakdowns of countries, investors, speakers and media, its venues. What you see has another public source, each one cited when you tap an edition. Each item comes back when we find one (we call these shadow sources: the same fact where we may read it).
- **Solid** columns were read in a public source other than websummit.com: a news agency's headline for 2024 (Lusa) and Portugal's trade agency for 2025. **Paler** ones come from Wikipedia's table (2016 to 2018, and about 70,000 for 2022, a rounder figure than the organiser's own). **n/a** means no figure we may show: 2019 and 2021 had none found, 2020 was held online, and 2023 had only the organiser's own.
- The sources do not always agree: 2025's 71,386 is below 2024's 71,528, so "record attendance" does not hold for 2025; the record the organiser claims for 2025 is for investors (1,857, up 74%). One recap gives 71,368.
- **Tap** an edition for its dates, figure and source (new tab). **Save as PNG** downloads the picture as it is now.
