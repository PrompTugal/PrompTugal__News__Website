## In short

- The health service pays a provider 200 euros for 12 months per treated patient for remote physiotherapy at home, the gazette's standard terms say. It covers all the sessions the treatment needs.
- A later programme for a different part of the body, after a new referral, pays 100 euros.
- Seven groups of conditions qualify, from back pain to stroke and speech therapy.
- Where a provider's device uses AI, the terms keep the final clinical decision with the health service's own doctor.

## The story

![A card: what Portugal's health service pays for remote physiotherapy. 200 euros for 12 months per patient treated, from the standard terms in the official gazette, February 2026.](https://promptugal.news/design/infographics/out/health-service-pays-remote-physiotherapy--paper--card.png?v=2026-11-19)

*The figure as the standard terms give it (Aviso n.º 3864/2026/2). Cover by reviewer@PrompTugal.vicky.*

The government's launch of remote physiotherapy in June gave no price (see [AI physiotherapy in Portugal's health service](ai-physiotherapy-portugal-health-service.html)). The official gazette does.

**The price.** The standard terms, published on 23 February 2026 as a convention open to providers, set a base value of 200 euros for 12 months per treated patient. It covers every session the prescribed programme needs for the body areas the doctor names, and unlimited sessions within the 12 months if recovery needs them. If treatment stops early, for example because the patient gives up or gets worse, the provider is paid only for the months used.

A patient who needs treatment for a different body area during those 12 months is referred again by a health service doctor. The provider then receives 100 euros for another 12 months. An order published on 9 February 2026 carries the same two amounts as its price table, for an initial and a later 12-month programme. The order prints no currency, and the terms give euros.

Observador reported in June a price of 200 euros fixed for a year, from a Sword Health vice-president. The gazette confirms the figure. The government's item says patients pay nothing.

**Who qualifies.** Patients referred by their health service doctor who can use the equipment on their own. Annex III of the terms lists seven groups of conditions:

- long-lasting muscle and joint problems
- sudden ones, such as sprains and stable injuries after a fracture or orthopaedic surgery
- pelvic floor problems
- heart and lung rehabilitation
- neurological conditions, such as stroke
- cognitive rehabilitation
- speech therapy

The government's launch item named only common muscle and joint problems.

**What a provider must do.** Sessions must be available around the clock, with a clinical team reachable from 08:00 to 20:00 on working days. A specialist doctor in physical and rehabilitation medicine supervises the treatment. The device must reach the patient's home within five working days of the patient contacting the provider, and it must be a class IIa medical device, a medium-risk class under EU rules. The provider must run a documented quality system.

**The AI rules.** The terms regulate AI only if a provider's device or platform includes it. The device's manual must then state the AI system's accuracy and limits. Patient data stays the property of the health unit or the patient. It may be used to train or improve AI only with the patient's opt-in consent, and then only after irreversible anonymisation.

The final validation of results belongs to the health service's own specialist doctor, even when the data came from AI. The terms call the AI a decision-support tool. Where the device's data and the clinical assessment disagree, the human assessment prevails for discharge or extension.

The convention lasts 12 months and renews itself unless a party objects 60 days before the end.

## What this does not claim

- **These are the state's prices and rules, not a record of payments.** The newsroom found no figure for what any provider has been paid or how many patients are treated, and no source says which providers have joined.
- **The list of conditions is the convention's, not any provider's offer.** Which conditions a provider treats is not stated.
- **Parts of the terms were not read.** The newsroom read the notice, clauses 1 to 13 (the first paragraph of 13 only), 19 to 23, 26 and 27, and annexes III and IV. It did not read clauses 14 to 18, 24 and 25, or annexes I and II.
- **The AI rules apply only where AI is present.** Whether a provider's device uses AI comes from the government's launch item, not from the terms.
