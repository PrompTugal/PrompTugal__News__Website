> **Update, 21 November 2026.** This article gave the Parliament's and the Commission's descriptions. On 12 May 2026, the law firm [William Fry](https://www.williamfry.com/knowledge/eu-ai-act-omnibus-deal-reached-postponed-deadlines-watermarking-compromise-and-the-nudificiation-prohibition/) wrote its reading of the provisional agreement of 7 May, before the Act was adopted. William Fry says:
>
> - **Why the delay.** The reason is operational, not political. The standards that providers rely on were not expected to be complete until late 2026, and some Member States were slow to name their national authorities.
> - **Products.** Only machinery leaves the high-risk regime. Medical devices, toys and other product types stay in it. The Commission is to set by delegated act, a rule it adopts itself, how the AI rules fit with those products' own approval procedures, within a period running to August 2027.
> - **Marking.** The duty in Article 50, the Act's rule that AI-generated audio, image, video and text carry a machine-readable mark, starts on 2 December 2026. The code of practice, a voluntary guide on how to comply written under the AI Office, was still a draft in May.
> - **Image and video tools.** Providers and deployers of generative image and video tools are expected to show controls, in design and in use, against foreseeable misuse to make the content the new ban covers.
> - **Registration.** Providers must register high-risk systems in the EU database, the EU's public register of such systems, even if they think their system is exempt.
> - **Sandboxes.** The deadline for national authorities to set up regulatory sandboxes, supervised test spaces for AI, is postponed.
>
> This is a law firm's reading of a provisional text. We have not read the final Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, and the firm's statements about standards and the code of practice are as of May. They may have changed.

## In short

- The EU changed its AI Act through the Digital Omnibus on AI, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744.
- The European Parliament says it was published in the Official Journal on 24 July 2026. The European Commission says it entered into force on 27 July 2026.
- High-risk AI rules now apply from 2 December 2027, or 2 August 2028 for AI built into products.
- The Parliament says a new ban covers AI that generates non-consensual intimate content and child sexual abuse material.
- The Commission says the delay exists because standards were late.

The European [Commission](../companies/european-commission/index.html) proposed the changes on 19 November 2025. The Parliament's Legislative Train page, updated on 25 October 2026, gives the path from there. The Parliament and the Council reached a provisional agreement on 7 May 2026. The Parliament's plenary approved it on 16 June, with 423 votes for, 57 against and 174 abstentions. The Council adopted it on 29 June. It was signed on 8 July and published in the Official Journal on 24 July.

The Commission's questions and answers, updated on 20 October 2026, give another date. It says the Omnibus entered into force on 27 July 2026. The two pages give different kinds of date, so both are given here as theirs.

### The dates

The Parliament and the Commission both give the new dates for high-risk AI systems. These are systems that the AI Act treats as a risk to people's safety or rights, such as AI used to decide who gets a job or a loan, says the Commission. They apply from 2 December 2027. AI built into products, such as medical devices, toys and lifts, follows on 2 August 2028. The rules had been due on 2 August 2026.

The Commission says why. The rules lean on standards: voluntary technical specifications that tell companies what to do and tell authorities what to check. They were not ready, and the Commission says this put the 2 August 2026 date in jeopardy. It says the extra time is also for Member States to set up the national authorities that oversee the rules. For the first of those standards, see our earlier article on [EN 18286](ai-act-first-harmonised-standard.html).

The Parliament adds a second date. The duty to mark AI-generated content is postponed to 2 December 2026 for systems placed on the market before 2 August 2026.

### What the Parliament says was added or changed

- **A new ban.** AI practices that generate non-consensual intimate or sexual content, and child sexual abuse material, are prohibited.
- **Registration.** A simplified duty to register applies to AI systems that are not high-risk.
- **Special-category personal data.** Using it to correct bias in AI stays limited by a strict necessity test.
- **Documentation.** The simpler paperwork for medium-sized companies is kept.
- **The AI Office.** Its powers are stronger. This is the Commission's body that enforces the Act's rules for general-purpose AI models, according to the Commission.
- **Machinery.** AI-enabled machinery leaves the Act's direct high-risk regime. It follows the Machinery Regulation, which the Commission must amend with health and safety requirements for such AI.

The Parliament's page also says what the Commission first proposed in 2025: linking the dates to the availability of standards. In the final text, according to the Parliament, the dates are fixed instead.

## What this does not claim

- **Two descriptions, not the law.** This article rests on the Parliament's page and the Commission's questions and answers. The legal text was not read. Where the two pages differ, each is credited.
- **The Commission page was read for the timeline and standards only.** It is a general guide to the AI Act, not an account of the Omnibus.
- **No costs, savings or company effects are given.** Neither page was read for them.
- **Dates can be read two ways.** The Parliament gives 24 July as publication and the Commission gives 27 July as entry into force. No page here says how the two relate.
- **Not legal advice.** Whether a system is high-risk depends on the Act's annexes. This article judges none.
