## In short

- A pilot called the AI and Robotics Action Plan, part of INOVA ALGARVE 3.0, offered consulting and a personal plan to 15 Algarve tourism firms. Applications ran from 20 February to 30 April 2026.
- At the first meeting of the regional tourism innovation platform on 18 March, the president of Algarve Evolution said agentic AI can search, compare and book tourism services, and told firms to prepare their digital channels for it.
- Turismo do Algarve ran a free four-hour AI bootcamp for tourism staff in Portimão on 17 April.
- Since 12 October, Algarve Evolution has been running a five-minute survey on how local firms use AI, to be repeated twice a year.

## The story

Tourism is the Algarve's main business, so it is where the region has aimed its AI help first. The AI and Robotics Action Plan is a pilot inside INOVA ALGARVE 3.0, a project led by the regional business association NERA with Algarve Evolution, the University of Algarve, the regional tourism body, the regional development commission CCDR Algarve, KIPT and Tertúlia Algarvia, co-funded by the ALGARVE 2030 programme. Barlavento reported on 23 February that it would select 15 tourism small and medium firms, each getting a digital-maturity check, a review of AI and robotics opportunities and a personal action plan. Algarve Evolution's project page now lists a first group of 15 selected firms and a second group of five more.

The sharpest warning came at the first meeting of CCDR Algarve's tourism innovation platform on 18 March, reported by Barlavento on 20 March. The president of Algarve Evolution said so-called agentic AI can search, compare and book tourism services on a traveller's behalf, and urged firms to prepare their digital channels for dealing with AI systems, not only with people. A University of Algarve tourism researcher at the same meeting added that today's tourism success can hide weaknesses, such as dependence on foreign demand.

Training followed the money. In March, Turismo do Algarve announced two free, certified bootcamps under its Competências do Futuro Algarve programme. The AI one, on 17 April at the hotel and tourism school in Portimão, covered ChatGPT, Gemini and Copilot, task automation and prompting for business use.

To learn what firms actually do, Algarve Evolution opened the Algarve Business AI Adoption Survey on 12 October. It asks how firms of any size use AI, what blocks them and where they see opportunity, reports answers only in aggregate and plans to run twice a year. We have not found results yet.

One firm has gone further than a plan. In April the Vale do Lobo resort in Loulé launched an AI concierge called Oliver that answers guests, owners and prospective buyers around the clock in several languages and recommends properties.

What we could not confirm: the amount of funding for the pilot, which firms were selected, and what any of them changed. The pilot's calls are closed, so the next test is whether the results are published.
