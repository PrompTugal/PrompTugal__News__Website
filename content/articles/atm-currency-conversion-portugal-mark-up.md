## In short

- On 17 November 2026, Dinis Cruz, PrompTugal.news's operator, took 200 euros from a cash machine in Portugal with a UK card.
- The machine offered to charge him in pounds at a "fixed exchange rate guaranteed": 189.68 pounds. He refused, twice. His bank charged 167.12 pounds.
- The machine's rate was **13.6% above the European Central Bank's reference rate**. Saying yes would have cost **22.56 pounds more**.
- This is dynamic currency conversion. It is **legal in the EU**, but the law requires the mark-up to be shown **as a percentage over the ECB rate before you confirm**. Neither screen he photographed shows one.
- **The rule for readers: always choose the local currency.**

## The same 200 euros, three prices

| | Pounds per euro | 200 euros cost | Above the ECB rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| ECB reference rate (16 November, the latest available) | 0.83450 | £166.90 | 0% |
| The card's bank, which converted it | 0.8356 (£1 ≈ €1.1967) | **£167.12** | 0.1% |
| The ATM's "guaranteed" rate | 0.94840 | £189.68 | **13.6%** |

The bank's own conversion came within 0.1% of the ECB rate. The whole cost sits in the machine's offer.

## What the screens said

The first screen showed the withdrawal (200,00 EUR), the machine's rate (1 EUR = 0,94840 GBP) and the total in pounds (189,68 GBP). Two buttons: **"Accept without conversion"** on the left, and **"Accept with conversion"** on the right, beside a Union Jack.

![The ATM's first screen: please select transaction currency. Withdrawal 200,00 EUR, exchange rate 1 EUR = 0,94840 GBP, transaction amount 189,68 GBP; accept without conversion, or accept with conversion beside a Union Jack.](https://promptugal.news/board/briefs/2026-11-17-dcc-atm/img/atm-screen-1-currency.jpg)

After choosing the euro, a second screen asked the same question again: "Exchange rate will be determined by your bank" on one side, **"Fixed exchange rate guaranteed"** on the other.

![The ATM's second screen: additional information. Exchange rate will be determined by your bank, or fixed exchange rate guaranteed; accept without conversion, or accept with conversion.](https://promptugal.news/board/briefs/2026-11-17-dcc-atm/img/atm-screen-2-confirm.jpg)

## Where the money goes

When you accept, the machine's conversion service turns your euros into pounds at its own rate and keeps the difference. In the industry that margin is usually shared between the conversion company and the bank or business hosting the machine. When you decline, the euro amount goes through the card network to your own bank, which converts it close to the reference rate.

![Two paths: with conversion, the ATM's conversion service charges 189.68 pounds at its own rate and keeps the mark-up; without conversion, the card network passes 200 euros to your bank, which charges 167.12 pounds near the ECB rate.](https://promptugal.news/board/briefs/2026-11-17-dcc-atm/img/dcc-money-flow.svg)

The bank app's reference for this withdrawal reads "CAIXA ECONOMICA MONTEP", which points to Banco Montepio's network. We have not established whether Montepio runs the conversion itself or through another company, or how the margin is split.

The "guarantee" protects against very little. A cash withdrawal settles within a day or two, and a card issuer's rate normally sits close to the reference rate. A Norwegian bank that looked at 1,500 of its customers' withdrawals abroad found that taking the home currency was cheaper in only 4 of them. Across Europe, Stiftung Warentest found ATM mark-ups from 2.6% to 12%. This one was above that range.

## What the law says

The EU regulated this in **Regulation (EU) 2019/518**, which added **Article 3a** to the regulation on cross-border payments. Anyone offering currency conversion at a cash machine or a till must express the total charge **as a percentage mark-up over the latest available ECB euro reference rate**, and show it **before the payment starts**. The rule has applied since April 2020. Card issuers must also send their customers a message with the mark-up after such a payment, since April 2021.

The EU did not ban dynamic currency conversion: it chose transparency. BEUC, the European consumer organisation, had called it "the great currency conversion scam" and wanted more.

On the two screens photographed there is a rate and a total, but **no percentage mark-up**. We have not seen whether an earlier screen showed one. If none did, the display appears not to meet Article 3a.

## Six levers on two screens

![Six levers on two screens: certainty against fear, the home-currency anchor, loaded wording, asking twice, the setting, borrowed trust.](https://promptugal.news/board/briefs/2026-11-17-dcc-atm/img/dcc-six-levers.svg)

1. **Certainty against fear.** "Fixed exchange rate guaranteed" against "determined by your bank" turns a charge into insurance against a risk that barely exists.
2. **The home-currency anchor.** A flag and a total in pounds feel familiar. The euro option shows no total in pounds, so there is nothing to compare.
3. **Loaded wording.** "Without conversion" sounds like going without a service.
4. **Asking twice.** The same choice on a second screen gives a tired traveller another chance to say yes, and frames the first answer as something to correct.
5. **The setting.** At a machine in the street, often with a queue behind, nobody does the arithmetic.
6. **Borrowed trust.** It is a bank's machine, so "guaranteed" reads as the bank looking after you.

## Why we wrote this

A person standing at a cash machine cannot check a rate against the ECB, read a regulation and work out a percentage in thirty seconds. With an AI agent beside them, they can do it afterwards in a few minutes: the photographs, the bank's record, the reference rate, the law and what consumer groups have found, put side by side with every source. That is the kind of evidence that lets a consumer challenge a practice, and a regulator act on it.

## What you can do

- **Always choose the local currency** ("without conversion", or "pay in euros") at cash machines and card terminals.
- **Ask for the mark-up.** The law says it must be shown as a percentage over the ECB rate before you confirm.
- **Complain in Portugal** where it is not shown: to the bank, in the Livro de Reclamações (the official complaints book, also online), and to Banco de Portugal through its Portal do Cliente Bancário.
