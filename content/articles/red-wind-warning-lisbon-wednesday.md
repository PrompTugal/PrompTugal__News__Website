## In short

- [Web Summit](../organisations/web-summit/index.html) [says](https://websummit.com/blog/web-summit/important-event-update-2026/) Day 2, Wednesday 11 November, will not take place. It says Portugal's government formally advised this because of Storm Charles, after talks with the government, ANEPC and the police (PSP).
- On Wednesday, Web Summit [said](https://websummit.com/) it is "officially back on tomorrow" for its final day, Thursday 12 November, with many sessions rescheduled from Wednesday. It said to check [the schedule](https://websummit.com/schedule/date/2026-11-12/) later on Wednesday for updates.
- [IPMA](../organisations/ipma/index.html), Portugal's weather institute, lowered its wind warning for the Lisbon district on Wednesday morning, from red to orange, then shortened it on Wednesday afternoon, and has since lifted it: its data at 16:39 UTC (modified at 16:15) showed no wind warning for Lisbon, and still did at 19:48 UTC (modified at 19:15). At 14:08 UTC it had shown orange until 18:00, with gusts up to 90 km/h and up to 110 km/h in the most exposed places. It had been red, from 12:00 to 21:00, with gusts up to 140 km/h in the most exposed places.
- Only the sea warning remains for Lisbon: IPMA's data at 19:48 UTC (modified at 19:15) shows orange for rough seas until 21:00, then yellow until 03:00 on Thursday. The rain warning ended at 18:00: IPMA's red rain warning had ended at 15:00, followed by yellow until 18:00. It had been red from 09:00 to 18:00.
- Portugal's government has declared an [alert situation](https://portugal.gov.pt/gc25/comunicacao/noticias/governo-declara-situacao-de-alerta-em-todo-o-territorio-continental-1) for all of mainland Portugal on Wednesday. Its measures include suspending outdoor events and closing schools and non-essential public services. The Internal Administration minister [said](https://portugal.gov.pt/pt/gc25/comunicacao/noticias/mau-tempo-a-proatividade-da-nos-tranquilidade-1) on Wednesday afternoon that keeping it on Thursday is not planned.
- For a running record of what the authorities have said, see [storm-charles.promptugal.news](https://storm-charles.promptugal.news) (not a warning service: follow IPMA and ANEPC).

## The story

Web Summit has called off Wednesday. In a statement on its website on Tuesday evening, ["Important event update"](https://websummit.com/blog/web-summit/important-event-update-2026/), also sent to attendees by email, it wrote: "The Government of Portugal has formally advised Web Summit that, due to the risks posed by Storm Charles, including tidal flooding, severe disruption to public transport and the impact of high winds on external structures, Day 2 of Web Summit should not take place." It said this followed discussions with the government, ANEPC and the Public Security Police (PSP), and that "Day 2 (Wednesday, November 11th) will not take place."

"Web Summit aims to proceed with Day 3 (Thursday, November 12) as planned, following the passing of Storm Charles," the statement said. Web Summit said it would keep monitoring conditions with the authorities, give further updates "as soon as confirmed information becomes available", and "communicate directly with those impacted." [Diário de Notícias](https://dn.pt/sociedade/proteo-civil-alerta-para-chuva-forte-e-rajadas-at-120-kmh-governo-pede-que-se-evitem-sadas-de-casa) reported the cancellation on its live page at 19:45, citing a note from the organisers that said the decision was coordinated directly with ANEPC and the PSP.

On Wednesday, Web Summit's [homepage](https://websummit.com/) announced: "Web Summit is officially back on tomorrow for our final day. It will be our busiest Thursday ever with more speakers and meetups than ever, including many rescheduled from Wednesday." It added a similar announcement to the top of its [statement](https://websummit.com/blog/web-summit/important-event-update-2026/), edited at 11:44 UTC, according to its blog's own data.

Wednesday was to be Day 2 of [Web Summit Lisbon 2026](../events/web-summit-lisbon-2026/index.html). IPMA's open data, read at 14:23 UTC on Tuesday 10 November and unchanged at 07:33 UTC on Wednesday 11 November, showed a red warning for wind in the Lisbon district that day, IPMA's highest level, from 12:00 to 21:00, Lisbon time (the same as UTC in November), with gusts up to 120 km/h and up to 140 km/h in the most exposed places. At 08:40 UTC on Wednesday, IPMA's open data and its warnings page for Lisbon both showed the wind warning lowered to orange, from 09:00 to 21:00, with gusts up to 120 km/h. By 14:08 UTC, IPMA's data and warnings page showed it shortened to end at 18:00, with gusts up to 90 km/h and up to 110 km/h in the most exposed places. At 16:39 UTC, IPMA's data (modified at 16:15) and its warnings page showed no wind warning for Lisbon. In its [third statement](https://www.ipma.pt/opencms/pt/otempo/comunicados/index.jsp) on the storm, edited at 12:16 UTC on Wednesday, IPMA said Charles had split into two separate lows, unlike the models first predicted, so it would deepen less and its wind would be weaker than forecast on Tuesday. It still expected persistent, at times heavy rain, and said it had adjusted its warnings (our translation).

Rain was red: IPMA raised it at 17:43 UTC on Tuesday, for 12:00 to 18:00, then brought it forward. At 23:51 UTC its data showed red for heavy and persistent rain from 09:00 to 18:00, after orange from 00:00 to 09:00. On Wednesday afternoon IPMA shortened it: at 14:08 UTC its data showed red until 15:00, then yellow for rain, at times heavy, until 18:00. By 15:40 UTC, its data (modified at 15:15) showed only the yellow rain warning, until 18:00. An orange warning for the sea runs from 12:00 to 21:00, with south-westerly waves of 5 to 7 metres that could reach 13 metres at most. The yellow rain warning ended at 18:00 as scheduled: at 19:48 UTC, IPMA's data (modified at 19:15) showed only the sea warning for Lisbon, orange until 21:00, then yellow, for south-westerly waves of 4 to 5 metres, until 03:00 on Thursday.

Portugal's government has [declared](https://portugal.gov.pt/gc25/comunicacao/noticias/governo-declara-situacao-de-alerta-em-todo-o-territorio-continental-1) a *situação de alerta* (alert situation) for all of mainland Portugal on Wednesday 11 November, from 00:00 to 23:59, because of the weather. Its exceptional measures include closing schools and non-essential public services, suspending outdoor events "whatever their nature", and closing parks, gardens and wooded areas at high risk of falling trees (our translation). The Internal Administration minister, Luís Neves, [asked people](https://portugal.gov.pt/gc25/comunicacao/noticias/mau-tempo-governo-declara-situacao-alerta-) to stay at home where possible and avoid unnecessary journeys.

Portugal's civil protection authority, ANEPC, has [raised its special readiness state](https://prociv.gov.pt/pt/noticias/depressao-charles-anepc-avisa-populacao-por-sms/) and sent an SMS warning to the population about the strong wind and persistent rain on Wednesday.

In an [update on the government's site](https://portugal.gov.pt/pt/gc25/comunicacao/noticias/mau-tempo-protecao-civil-apela-a-adocao-de-medidas-de-seguranca) at 13:31 on Wednesday, ANEPC said the change in Charles's track had reduced the forecast impact on the Lisbon region and increased the exposure of the Alentejo and the Algarve. Between midnight and midday it recorded 538 incidents, mostly fallen trees, landslides, floods and road clearing, with Lisbon and Setúbal the most affected. Its special readiness state, at level 4, stays in force across the country (our translation).

In a [briefing](https://portugal.gov.pt/pt/gc25/comunicacao/noticias/mau-tempo-a-proatividade-da-nos-tranquilidade-1) at ANEPC, reported on the government's site at 16:49 on Wednesday, the Internal Administration minister, Luís Neves, said the alert situation stays in force until 23:59 on Wednesday and that keeping it on Thursday 12 November is not planned, though repair work may be needed and some services may stay limited (our translation).
