## In short

- In 2025 and 2026, several free programmes have aimed to bring more women into AI in Portugal. They include an online course, a certified course for 500 women, a build day and a forum.
- Portuguese Women in Tech, a community founded in 2016, runs mentoring and programmes for female founders.
- Portuguese press reports that 14% of Portuguese startups are founded or led by women, and that few women say they want to start a tech business.
- Most of this is learning and building with AI. It is not a list of women who have built AI companies in Portugal.

PME Magazine reports that 14% of Portuguese startups are founded or led by women. It also reports a Mastercard study of March 2025. In it, 57% of women in Portugal described themselves as entrepreneurs. Only 4% named technology and IT as the field they would start a business in, against 15% of men. Here is what is on offer.

**A free online course.** On 12 March 2026, Human Resources reported that the APDC had joined Microsoft Portugal and Founderz to bring AI Skills 4 Women to Portugal. The programme is online and free. It aims to build digital skills and bring more women closer to AI opportunities.

**A certified course for 500 women.** In September 2025, Executiva reported that BORA Mulheres was launching its first Academia, on AI. BORA Mulheres is a free entrepreneurship programme run by Coca-Cola Europacific Partners and Impact Hub Lisbon. The certified online course was due to run from October to November 2025, in four weekly modules. It covered AI in everyday use, ethics, critical thinking and job-market trends, in Portuguese with Portuguese Sign Language interpretation. Executiva reports that the programme has had seven editions and more than 5,200 entrepreneurs enrolled since 2019.

**A day of building.** W35, a community, planned Build Lab for 27 October in Lisbon, Porto and Faro. PME Magazine reported beforehand that about 50 people were expected in each city. After a first part on deciding what to build, participants were to have 90 minutes to make a first prototype with AI tools. Lovable was to give each of them 100 credits. No coding or AI experience was needed.

**A forum and an evening.** The 2nd International Women in Digital Forum was scheduled for the morning of 13 November in Lisbon. MUDA runs it with Women in Tech Portugal. Human Resources reports three panels on women's leadership in the age of AI, free with registration. In May, API (Artificial Portugal Intelligence) held Women & Allies Shaping AI in Portugal in Porto. Its page describes a panel and small-group evening on moving AI from pilots to practice, with speakers from enterprise, healthcare and people (HR) roles.

**A longer-running community.** Portuguese Women in Tech says it was founded in April 2016 by Liliana Castro and Inês Santos Silva. Its own page lists mentoring, entrepreneurship programmes for female founders, an awards scheme, a hackathon and a salary-transparency project.

**One founder with a Lisbon link.** Defined.ai supplies data for AI. Its page says Daniela Braga founded it in 2015 in Seattle. Its link to Portugal is its research and development centre in Lisbon and, it says, an office in Porto.

## What this does not claim

- **This is not a map of women who have built AI products in Portugal.** Most items here are training, events and community. This newsroom has not counted women founders, researchers or engineers.
- **Press figures are as the press reports them.** The 14%, the Mastercard 57%, 4% and 15%, and the expected 50 a city come from PME Magazine. The Mastercard study itself was not read.
- **No outcomes are claimed.** No source read here says whether Build Lab took place, how the forum went, or how many women finished any course or built something lasting.
- **The APDC's role is as Human Resources reports it.** This newsroom has not read the APDC's own page.
- **Women in Tech Portugal and Portuguese Women in Tech are not shown to be the same organisation.**
- **Defined.ai is not claimed to be a Portuguese company.** Whether Daniela Braga still leads it is not checked here.
- **Nothing here covers Web Summit's own gender report.**
