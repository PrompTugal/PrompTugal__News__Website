## In short

- On 25 November 2026 the European Commission proposed a Quantum Act, a framework to turn Europe's quantum research into products and services, strengthen supply chains and prepare for post-quantum cryptography.
- The proposal also lays the groundwork for a Sovereign Tech Equity Fund, a new investment vehicle that would provide equity and similar financing for technologies the Commission calls essential to Europe's technological sovereignty and economic security.
- The trade site Tech.eu headlined the fund as 50 billion euros and its text has the Commission saying "the proposed €50 billion fund". The Commission's press release, news article and explainer do not give that figure, and the explainer says no fixed budget is allocated under the proposal.
- It is a proposal. The European Parliament and the Council now consider it.

## The story

The Commission describes the Quantum Act as resting on three pillars: research excellence, bringing quantum technology into use, and keeping systems and supplies secure. It would be delivered through a Quantum Europe Framework Initiative that brings EU and national programmes together around shared priorities.

On research and industry, the proposal includes a new network of leading European research institutes, "Grand Challenges" (funding competitions aimed at bottlenecks such as quantum computers that keep working when errors occur, and navigation devices that work without satellite signals), and pan-European quantum infrastructures such as quantum computers, quantum simulators and quantum-enhanced medical imaging. Technology and innovation hubs would help start-ups and small firms develop market-ready products.

On security, the Commission and Member States would monitor supply chains, identify critical suppliers and components and reduce supply-chain risks. The Act would also support a coordinated move to post-quantum cryptography, with preparedness monitoring and targeted procurement safeguards.

The Act "allows for the establishment" of the Sovereign Tech Equity Fund, which the Commission says will provide equity and similar types of financing and will help fund the Quantum Europe Framework Initiative and measures in its Tech Sovereignty Package. It says it will work with the European Investment Bank Group and Member States to establish the fund quickly. In its explainer, the Commission says the aim should be for the fund to be operational by the start of the next multiannual financial framework, the EU's long-term budget.

## What the 50 billion euros is, and is not

Tech.eu's article, "EU proposes €50BN equity fund to support quantum and essential sovereign tech", says the EU "has proposed a €50 billion equity fund" and, in its text, that "the Commission said the proposed €50 billion fund will provide equity". We read the Commission's press release, its news article and its "Quantum Act explained" page, and none of them states 50 billion euros. The explainer says: "While no fixed budget is allocated under the proposal, the proposal empowers the Commission to establish a new Sovereign Tech Equity Fund". The press release adds that future EU funding under the Act "would depend on budget decisions and the rules of the programmes concerned".

We could not find where the 50 billion euro figure comes from. Tech.eu attributes a separate point, that the fund would take stakes in European firms, to Politico, which we did not read. Until a source gives the size, treat 50 billion euros as the trade site's figure, not the Commission's.

For scale, the Commission's background says the 2025 Quantum Europe Strategy reported more than 11 billion euros of public quantum investment by the EU and its Member States.

## What this does not claim

- **This is a proposal, not law.** The Parliament and the Council have not adopted it, and the text may change.
- **No fund size is established.** See above.
- **Nothing here says what the Act means for Portugal.** We did not find a Portuguese reaction or a Portuguese project named in the Commission's pages.
- We did not read the draft legal text, only the Commission's summaries.
