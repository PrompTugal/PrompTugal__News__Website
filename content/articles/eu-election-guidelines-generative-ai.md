## In short

- The European Commission has opened a consultation on revised election guidelines under the Digital Services Act. It runs from 10 to 23 November 2026.
- The guidelines are for the largest online platforms and search engines, those with at least 45 million monthly users in the EU.
- The draft asks them to label deepfakes clearly, and to keep the label when the content is shared.
- It also asks them to test their recommender systems before every national election, and to report to the Commission within 30 days of a vote.
- Anyone with relevant experience and expertise can respond, through an online form.

## The story

The [European Commission](../companies/european-commission/index.html) wants to update its rules of thumb for elections online, with generative AI in mind. On 10 November it opened a targeted consultation on draft revised election guidelines under the Digital Services Act (DSA). Views are invited until 23 November 2026.

The first guidelines date from April 2024. They tell the largest platforms and search engines how to reduce risks to elections, under Article 35 of the DSA. The Commission says the update responds to new technology and to "increasingly sophisticated manipulation risks". It also answers a call from the European Council for a swift revision.

The draft proposes five main changes, according to the Commission. Platforms should detect coordinated inauthentic behaviour early and dismantle the networks behind it. They should spot suspicious trends before they go viral, and test their algorithms for manipulation before every national election. They should keep a record of changes to their recommender systems in the six months before a vote.

On AI, the draft is detailed. Deepfakes should be clearly labelled, so that a user sees the label at once, without having to do anything. The label should stay when others share the content. If someone downloads it or sends it through another service, the platform should not strip the embedded marking and should keep a visible label. Platforms that run their own generative AI tools should mark the outputs in a machine-readable way, as the AI Act requires, and test those tools against election scenarios. Illegal deepfakes of candidates should come down fast, the Commission says.

Candidates would get dedicated channels to the platforms, with threats, harassment and impersonation handled quickly and neutrally. After each election, platforms would send a review report to the Commission and the national regulator within 30 days, and publish a public version, the Commission says. The report would cover incidents, response times and AI labelling.

The Commission stresses that the guidelines are content-neutral. The draft says nothing in them suggests removing, demoting or labelling lawful content because of the political opinion it expresses. Platforms may use other measures if they show these work as well or better.

Startups building AI tools for detection, provenance or content labelling could find this consultation worth reading, and so could civil society groups working on elections. At Web Summit, they might compare notes with the platforms' policy teams.

## What this does not claim

- **A draft, not the rules.** These are proposed guidelines. The Commission may change them after the consultation.
- **Who is covered.** The draft applies to platforms and search engines the Commission has designated as very large. This article does not list them.
- **No reactions.** We have not seen any platform's response to the draft, and none is quoted.
