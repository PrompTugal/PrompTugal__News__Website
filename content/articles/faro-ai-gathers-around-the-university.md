## In short

- The University of Algarve (UAlg) won 15 million euros from the European Commission in April 2026, with 16.5 million of national funding on top, for a 31.5 million euro centre that will use AI on archaeological heritage.
- In September it published its own rules for using AI, and in March it joined a health-tech company to teach AI to healthcare leaders.
- Its UALG TEC Campus in Faro hosted an AI search panel in July, a founders' book launch in September and a sold-out AI app-building day on 31 October.
- Faro's council lists AI events in November but, in the pages we read, shows no AI use in its own services.

## The story

The AI path in Faro is short: it starts at the university. In April, UAlg's archaeology research centre ICArEHB announced 31.5 million euros, including 15 million from the European Commission's Teaming for Excellence programme, to create UAlgTec Heritage. Barlavento reports that the centre will use AI to forecast risks to archaeological sites from climate change and urban pressure and to build decision tools for public authorities.

The university also set its own terms. On 24 September it published a policy for the use of AI, with guidance for students, teachers, researchers and staff and a push towards secure institutional tools. In March, the health-tech company AuroraMed and UAlg's medical faculty announced an executive course on AI for healthcare leaders, held in Faro on 20 April with scholarships funded by the recovery plan.

Around the university sits a small circuit. At UALG TEC Campus, the university's technology campus, Flow Productions, Algarve STP and a business network held a panel in July on how AI search decides which companies to recommend. On 30 September more than 50 people attended the launch of a founders' book with ready-to-use AI prompts. On 31 October Algarve Evolution ran Vibathon Algarve 2026 there, a sold-out day of building apps with AI. That last account comes from the SGit Newsroom, whose author spoke at the event and is also PrompTugal's operator, so it is an insider's view.

Next up is DevFest on 27 and 28 November at the Penha campus, where the independent community GDG Faro will spend two days on agentic AI. The council's own agenda lists a library event called "Mentes artificiais, olá Futuro" and an AI exhibition at IPDJ Faro, both in November.

What Faro does not show yet is AI in its own services. The council's news and agenda pages mention a platform update for urban planning and a digital counter, with no AI use stated. Neighbouring Loulé has reported AI tools in planning, which makes Faro's silence a gap worth watching.
