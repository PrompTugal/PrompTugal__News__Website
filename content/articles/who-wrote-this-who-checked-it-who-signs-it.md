*Opinion, by Dinis Cruz, PrompTugal.news's operator. Dictated as a voice memo on 17 November 2026 and written up by conductor@PrompTugal.dinis, an AI agent, which also did the research on what other newsrooms do. Not yet signed by Dinis with his key.*

## In short

- An LLM can write five different articles from the same facts, and all five carry the same weight to the model. The human who picks one is exercising judgement. So **who wrote it, and with what prompt, is now as much a part of provenance as the sources**.
- Every piece on PrompTugal.news will say what it is (news, analysis, opinion, editorial, data), **who originated it, where its facts came from, who checked them and who signs it**.
- Every article will separate **facts linked to evidence, opinions stated as facts, and opinions stated as opinions**. A fact without evidence is an opinion.
- The people a story is about will be invited to answer, **on the record and signed**, on the article itself.

## The problem with "AI-generated"

On this site some articles were written by agents and published with no human reviewer. Others were reviewed by Vicky or by me, with editorial choices made. The ATM piece began with my own withdrawal, my photographs and my argument, but an AI did a great deal of the heavy lifting: the reference rate, the regulation, the studies. The payment desk piece is closer to a column I dictated. A label that says "AI-generated" tells you nothing about any of that.

Researchers and newsrooms have noticed the same thing. There is no shared standard for labelling AI use; bare labels lower trust without informing anyone; readers do tell "AI-assisted" from "AI-written". The Trust Project already asks newsrooms to label the type of work, news against opinion against analysis. The Paris Charter on AI and Journalism asks for the origin and traceability of content. Neither says how to show the work of a newsroom where most of the writing is done by agents.

## A profile, not a label

What I want is closer to a formula. Five things, each recorded with who and when:

1. **Origin:** did a human write it, dictate it, or brief an agent that drafted it? Did an agent write it from its own mandate, or did code produce it from data? Different, not better: a page of live weather data needs no opinion and little AI at all.
2. **Research:** none, the author's own, an AI's directed by the author, agents on their own, or a data pipeline.
3. **Claims:** how many facts, each with its evidence, and the evidence followed to where it actually says so. Opinions labelled as opinions.
4. **Review:** which agents and which models checked it, how many independent models agreed, which human edited it (with the diff), and whether the author signed this exact version.
5. **External:** signed responses from the people it is about, signed reviews from experts and readers, signed corrections.

And a ladder, like Wikipedia's assessment scale: published by an agent; sourced; verified by several models; signed by a human; and finally **on the record**, when the people it concerns have been asked and their answer is published.

## Sign it

I should be able to read an article and sign it with my key: "yes, I am behind this". My understanding is that in many news organisations what a reporter wrote can be changed by others along the way, headlines above all. Here the version I sign is the version you read, and any change after it is a signed difference, in public.

The same goes for the people we write about. If PrompTugal.news writes about a bank's cash machines, we should write to the bank: here is the article, would you like to respond? Their answer, signed by them, goes on the article. If they show we were wrong, we correct it, and the correction is signed and linked to their response.

## On the record, not in the comments

This is not a comments section. Anonymous comments are where people say things they would never say on the record. What I want is the opposite: two, three or four points of view on one article, each from someone willing to put their name and their key to it, each backed by their own evidence if they have it. They may use an AI to do their research, as I do. That is fine: it is on the record. Civility can be checked. Some of those contributions should be paid, and labelled as paid.

Once those views are there, anyone can ask an AI to collate them. The article itself can grow: "these three responses make these points, with this evidence". Sometimes the evidence will contradict itself, especially about the future. That is fine too. What matters is that it is mapped.

## A newspaper, organised as one

News is not opinion. "I went to a cash machine and was offered a 13.6% mark-up" is news. What I think about it is analysis and opinion. PrompTugal.news will be organised like a newspaper: news, local news, data, analysis, opinion, a short editorial every day at the top, and a place for what is said on the record. I am one of its authors.

This piece is itself an example. It began as a voice memo. An agent turned my rambling into a line of argument and did the research on what others do. On the ladder above it is published by an agent, with its facts sourced in the plan behind it, and not yet signed. When I sign it, it moves up a rung, and you will be able to see that it did.
