> **Follow-up, 19 November 2026.** The gazette's terms set the price and the rules: [What the health service pays for remote physiotherapy at home](health-service-pays-remote-physiotherapy.html).

> **Update, 19 November 2026.** The [official gazette](https://files.diariodarepublica.pt/gratuitos/2s/2026/02/2S037A0000S00.pdf) published the standard terms of the service on 23 February 2026 (Aviso 3864/2026/2). They show it works through a convention, a standard contract between the health system's central administration and a provider, which any provider that meets its terms can join:
>
> - **Open to join.** Any person or company, with or without profit, that provides this kind of rehabilitation care and meets the terms can join, by applying with the forms attached to them. Joining a convention means accepting its published terms as they stand.
> - **Who it covers.** Health service patients whose doctor refers them and who have the digital skills to use the equipment on their own.
> - **AI.** The terms do not require it. If a provider's device or platform includes AI, they require it to document its accuracy and limits and to have a risk-management system. They also require that health professionals can interpret, override or reverse automated decisions.
> - **Sword Health Physio.** A letter dated 1 June 2026, on the letterhead of the health system's central administration, authorises it to join for 12 months, renewable. We found the letter online as [a copy](https://a.storyblok.com/f/325797/x/37d8fa1adf/autorizacao-celebracao-convencao-sword-health-sa.pdf) and have not checked it.
>
> We found no source saying whether any other provider has joined.

> **Update, 18 November 2026.** Two reports added to what we knew: a launch-day report in [DNOTICIAS](https://www.dnoticias.pt/2026/6/18/496006-medicos-vao-poder-prescrever-fisioterapeuta-em-casa-com-inteligencia-artificial/), published on 18 June 2026, and a [report from RTP](https://www.rtp.pt/noticias/pais/futura-uls-sao-joao-tera-projeto-piloto-de-telerreabilitacao-pos-cirurgica_n1538412), based on Lusa, dated 21 December 2023, which links an earlier pilot to the [Center for Responsible AI](center-for-responsible-ai-money-partners-goals.html).
>
> - **The nine units (DNOTICIAS).** The protocols were signed for the local health units of Santa Maria, Algarve, Alto Ave, Baixo Mondego, Cova da Beira, Matosinhos, São João, Almada Seixal and São José. The report says the model may extend to others that ask.
> - **The waiting-time figure (DNOTICIAS).** The report says Cova da Beira was the first unit to give patients the devices, in 2025. It says, citing the unit's board president, that the average wait to start physiotherapy there fell from two years to 10 days after hospital referral. Our [update on the Center for Responsible AI article](center-for-responsible-ai-money-partners-goals.html) gave the same figure as the Center's chief executive's claim about a Porto hospital.
> - **An earlier pilot (RTP).** It describes a different project from the 2026 agreement: a trial at São João in Porto for people recovering from surgery. Treatment was to begin in person and continue with physiotherapy at home. It was planned for six months and about 300 patients. The report says the project "was created out of" the Center. It names the São João hospital centre and Sword Health as members of the consortium.
> - **The pilot's supervision (RTP).** Progress was to be monitored on a Sword Health online platform, with alerts supervised by a rehabilitation doctor. The report does not say the platform used AI. Neither the government's item nor Observador mentions this pilot.
>
> The two accounts of the waiting-time figure differ. We have not checked either, and we do not say which is right. The 2023 report is about a planned pilot. It gives no results and no waiting-time figure. We do not say the pilot became the 2026 service.

## In short

- The government says the national health service (SNS) and Sword Health, a Portuguese company, agreed to offer remote physiotherapy. It was presented on 18 June 2026 and is free to patients.
- A doctor prescribes it. AI tools check and correct the patient's movements in real time, and health professionals supervise.
- The government expects a 97 percent shorter wait to start treatment and a saving of about 45 percent. Both are estimates.
- Nine local health units also signed protocols with Sword to explore AI in other care.

## The story

Physiotherapy at home, checked by AI, is joining Portugal's public health service, the government says.

On 18 June 2026, the Minister of Health and the Minister for State Reform presented the agreement at the Santa Maria local health unit in Lisbon, according to the government's news item. Observador reported the same event that day.

**How it works.** A doctor in the SNS prescribes a remote programme for common muscle and joint problems: low back, shoulder or knee pain, sprains and strains. A clinical team of rehabilitation doctors and physiotherapists first assesses the patient. The equipment then comes to the home. A certified medical device, equipment approved for medical use, and AI tools follow the exercises and correct the movements in real time. Health professionals supervise permanently, the government says.

Sword adds, as Observador reports, that patients register on its site after the prescription and that the device arrives in two days on average.

**The cost.** The government says patients pay nothing. Observador reports that the agreement sets 200 euros per patient in rehabilitation. A Sword vice-president told it the price is fixed for a year, with unlimited sessions. Sword's chief executive told reporters that the total cost to the SNS will depend on take-up. He estimated it at a fifth of the traditional system's cost.

**The estimates.** The government says the technology is expected to cut the wait to start treatment by 97 percent and to save about 45 percent compared with conventional physiotherapy. Observador attributes both figures to the company's forecast.

The service follows a government order of February 2026, Despacho 1211/2026, which allowed telerehabilitation, meaning rehabilitation at a distance, with certified medical devices into the SNS.

On the same occasion, nine local health units signed protocols with Sword to explore AI in other care. The government names triage, which sorts patients by urgency, management of continued care and mental health, among others.

## What this does not claim

- **No independent check.** The 97 percent, the 45 percent and the one fifth are forecasts. The newsroom found no measured result.
- **The start and take-up are not known.** The government's page said the service would be available "from next week". The newsroom found nothing on when it started, how many patients use it or how well it works.
- **The page's dates disagree.** It shows 19 June 2026 and "updated 24 June 2026", its text says the presentation was on 18 June, and a photo caption says protocols were signed on 18 May. Observador's report of 18 June matches the text.
- **The 200 euros is Observador's report** of what Sword said. The government's page gives no price.
- **No claim links this service to other Sword Health work in Portugal.** Neither source mentions the waiting-time claim in the [update to our Center for Responsible AI article](center-for-responsible-ai-money-partners-goals.html), and this article does not confirm it.
