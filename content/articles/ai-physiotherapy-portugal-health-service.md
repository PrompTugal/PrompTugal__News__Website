> **Update, 18 November 2026.** A [report from RTP](https://www.rtp.pt/noticias/pais/futura-uls-sao-joao-tera-projeto-piloto-de-telerreabilitacao-pos-cirurgica_n1538412), based on Lusa, dated 21 December 2023, links an earlier pilot to the [Center for Responsible AI](center-for-responsible-ai-money-partners-goals.html). It describes a different project from the 2026 agreement:
>
> - **The pilot.** A trial at São João in Porto for people recovering from surgery. Treatment was to begin in person and continue with physiotherapy at home. It was planned for six months and about 300 patients.
> - **The link.** The report says the project "was created out of" the Center. It names the São João hospital centre and Sword Health as members of the consortium.
> - **The supervision.** Progress was to be monitored on a Sword Health online platform, with alerts supervised by a rehabilitation doctor. The report does not say the platform used AI.
> - **The 2026 sources.** Neither the government's item nor Observador mentions this pilot.
>
> The 2023 report is about a planned pilot. It gives no results and no waiting-time figure. We do not say the pilot became the 2026 service.

## In short

- The government says the national health service (SNS) and Sword Health, a Portuguese company, agreed to offer remote physiotherapy. It was presented on 18 June 2026 and is free to patients.
- A doctor prescribes it. AI tools check and correct the patient's movements in real time, and health professionals supervise.
- The government expects a 97 percent shorter wait to start treatment and a saving of about 45 percent. Both are estimates.
- Nine local health units also signed protocols with Sword to explore AI in other care.

## The story

*The figure as the government's page gives it, an estimate. Cover by reviewer@PrompTugal.vicky.*

Physiotherapy at home, checked by AI, is joining Portugal's public health service, the government says.

On 18 June 2026, the Minister of Health and the Minister for State Reform presented the agreement at the Santa Maria local health unit in Lisbon, according to the government's news item. Observador reported the same event that day.

**How it works.** A doctor in the SNS prescribes a remote programme for common muscle and joint problems: low back, shoulder or knee pain, sprains and strains. A clinical team of rehabilitation doctors and physiotherapists first assesses the patient. The equipment then comes to the home. A certified medical device, equipment approved for medical use, and AI tools follow the exercises and correct the movements in real time. Health professionals supervise permanently, the government says.

Sword adds, as Observador reports, that patients register on its site after the prescription and that the device arrives in two days on average.

**The cost.** The government says patients pay nothing. Observador reports that the agreement sets 200 euros per patient in rehabilitation. A Sword vice-president told it the price is fixed for a year, with unlimited sessions. Sword's chief executive told reporters that the total cost to the SNS will depend on take-up. He estimated it at a fifth of the traditional system's cost.

**The estimates.** The government says the technology is expected to cut the wait to start treatment by 97 percent and to save about 45 percent compared with conventional physiotherapy. Observador attributes both figures to the company's forecast.

The service follows a government order of February 2026, Despacho 1211/2026, which allowed telerehabilitation, meaning rehabilitation at a distance, with certified medical devices into the SNS.

On the same occasion, nine local health units signed protocols with Sword to explore AI in other care. The government names triage, which sorts patients by urgency, management of continued care and mental health, among others.

## What this does not claim

- **No independent check.** The 97 percent, the 45 percent and the one fifth are forecasts. The newsroom found no measured result.
- **The start and take-up are not known.** The government's page said the service would be available "from next week". The newsroom found nothing on when it started, how many patients use it or how well it works.
- **The page's dates disagree.** It shows 19 June 2026 and "updated 24 June 2026", its text says the presentation was on 18 June, and a photo caption says protocols were signed on 18 May. Observador's report of 18 June matches the text.
- **The 200 euros is Observador's report** of what Sword said. The government's page gives no price.
- **No claim links this service to other Sword Health work in Portugal.** Neither source mentions the waiting-time claim in the [update to our Center for Responsible AI article](center-for-responsible-ai-money-partners-goals.html), and this article does not confirm it.
