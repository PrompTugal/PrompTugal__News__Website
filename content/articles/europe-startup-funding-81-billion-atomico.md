## In short

- Tech.eu reported on 18 November 2026 that Atomico's State of European Tech report expects 81 billion dollars invested in European startups this year, the second-highest level on record.
- Atomico estimates that a record 40,000 founders started new tech companies in Europe, up 36 per cent on a year earlier.
- "First-money-in" rounds, before Series A, fell almost 30 per cent between 2021 and 2025.
- Europe hosts 7 per cent of the world's computing capacity, against 72 per cent for the United States.

## The story

Atomico, a venture capital firm, publishes an annual survey of European technology. Tech.eu's account of the 2026 edition gives the headline figures: investment in European startups of 81 billion dollars this year, an ecosystem valued at 4.4 trillion dollars (up 18 per cent in a year), more than 450 companies worth over a billion dollars and 53 of them over 10 billion.

The report's argument, in Tech.eu's account, is that money is concentrating. The earliest rounds fell by almost 30 per cent between 2021 and 2025 while the totals are high. On computing, the report puts Europe at 7 per cent of the world's capacity and the United States at 72 per cent. On mood, 46 per cent of the people surveyed feel more positive about European tech than a year ago.

The report also argues that European households hold 50 trillion dollars in financial assets and that moving 1 per cent of it towards venture capital would add about 500 billion dollars. Tom Wehmeier, Atomico's partner and head of intelligence, is quoted: "The data on the European tech ecosystem stopped being ambiguous some time ago."

For Portugal, see our separate piece on AICEP's report of an Atomico estimate for the country. The Tech.eu article itself does not mention Portugal.

## What this does not claim

- **These are Atomico's figures, via Tech.eu.** We did not read the full report.
- **Atomico is a venture investor,** so the report is also an argument for more venture investment.
- **The 2026 total is a forecast** for the year, not a final count.
- Nothing here is about a particular Portuguese company.
