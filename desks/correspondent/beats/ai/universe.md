# The universe of AI agents at Web Summit Lisbon 2026

Working map for the journalist: ideas and evidence, not an article. Read through the publication's lens: **Reach** (what can the agent actually do?), **Mandate** (what was it asked to do?), **Gap** (who controls the difference?). The lens comes from RiskMandate; according to riskmandate.ai, reach is "Everything the agent can actually access in this deployment, including what nobody thought to check.", mandate is "The job, written down: what the business authorised it to do.", and gap is "Reach minus mandate." (https://www.riskmandate.ai, read 6 November 2026). Nothing more is claimed about RiskMandate here.

Method and caution: sessions come from the team's `data/events/` (re-checked at websummit.com by the programme desk; four session pages re-read by this desk on 6 November 2026). Company statements come from each company's own public pages, read 6 November 2026 through an ordinary page fetch whose summarising step paraphrases; quotes below are what the fetch returned and **must be re-read on the page before publication**. We report what a company says and what its page does not say; absence on a marketing page is not evidence that a control does not exist, and we do not judge any company's security.

## 3. Patterns

- **Reach is described by integration count or product category, not by permission.** Make counts integrations, PolyAI lists systems and use cases, Strawberry names apps. Few pages say what credential the agent holds or which actions are writes. Strawberry is the clearest on credentials (passwords encrypted on device).
- **Controls cluster in three kinds:** a human approval step (Make, PolyAI, GetVocal, Strawberry per app and action), visibility after the fact (Make's Reasoning panel, GetVocal's audit logs and Control Tower, Emergence's traceability), and compliance badges (SOC 2, ISO 27001, GDPR on nearly every page).
- **Compliance badges are about the vendor's data handling, not about what the agent may do.** The badges answer where data sits; they do not answer what the agent was authorised to do.
- **Mandate is rarely written down as a thing.** The nearest are "set clear rules" (Make), "grounded in your rules and never improvise" (GetVocal), "policies, permissions, and system boundaries" (Emergence).
- **Gap tooling is absent from the pages read.** No page described comparing what the agent can reach with what it was authorised to do. Absence on a marketing page is noted neutrally.
- **The programme is ahead of the product pages on risk language.** Sessions are titled "When AI agents go rogue", "Who let the agent in?", "Who takes the fall for autonomous AI?", "Double agents", "Your AI agent just got a passport", "(A)Identity", "An AI identity crisis", and a Gravitee press conference on "Collapsing confidence in agent AI security after six months of reality". The product pages mostly speak about capability.
- **Identity is the sub-theme with most sessions:** passport, identity crisis, (A)Identity, Beyond the front door, Who let the agent in, Delinea's "Trust issues". Several are hosted by identity vendors (Delinea, Descope, Persona, Sumsub, Deque).
- **Concentration:** the stages are Stage 13 (AI Summit) 11 and 12 Nov for the vision talks, Stage 8 (Cybersecurity) and Masterclasses for the controls talks, Centre Stage for the big-name framing. The control conversation is mostly on smaller stages and in vendor-hosted slots.
