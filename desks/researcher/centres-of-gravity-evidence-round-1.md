## Round 2 (wake of 16:17 UTC, read 2026-11-06)
- Microsoft: Web Summit's partner page shows a Microsoft logo and a testimonial only under "Some partners from our past events", with no tier. That is evidence of past partnership, not of 2026. [websummit.com/partners](https://websummit.com/partners/), read 2026-11-06.
- Amazon, AWS, Google and Google Cloud do not appear on that page. Google Cloud's 2026 listing is the event partners page already cited.
- Speakers: the public speakers page loads 20 entries at a time ("Load More"); none of the first 20 lists Amazon, AWS, Microsoft or Google as company. Not exhaustive. [websummit.com/speakers](https://websummit.com/speakers/).
- Our own data: among 264 people files, one names an employer in the three groups (Amazon, the press conference already cited); programme and curation files add nothing else. DevFest Lagos 2026 (7 Nov) lists Google Developer Groups as a partner (Algarve side, not Google Cloud).
- Reading: from what is public and reachable today the centres are thin on the summit's own pages: Google Cloud (partner, 2026), Amazon (one press conference), Microsoft (past editions only), AWS (nothing found). The stronger evidence will be per company (cloud marketplace listings, "built on", partner programmes), which needs each company's own page: 120 companies is a job for a mandate of its own, in batches. Proposal: ontologist@ gives me the 20 companies Dinis cares about most, I read each one's public partner and customer pages for the three clouds.

## Round 3, read 7 November 2026 (about 05:20 UTC): a search of the organiser's 2025 session summaries
A search restricted to websummit.com for 2025 sessions naming AWS, Google Cloud or Microsoft found no 2025 Lisbon session summary from any of the three; the summary pages it returned are on other topics (for example [Opening remarks](https://websummit.com/summaries/lis25/opening-remarks/), [The future of AI and machine learning](https://websummit.com/summaries/lis25/the-future-of-ai-and-machine-learning/)), whose full text I did not read. Microsoft appears as a partner on the organiser's Vancouver edition page, not Lisbon. The summaries live at `/summaries/lis25/<slug>/`. This is a search-engine result, a pointer and not a finding: absence of a hit does not mean absence of a session. The speaker lists by company (the speakers page loads more by script) remain the stronger route and are not yet read.


## Round 4, read 7 November 2026 (about 06:20 UTC): the 2026 speakers page, loaded in a browser
[websummit.com/speakers/](https://websummit.com/speakers/) (headed "2026 speakers") shows 20 speakers in a plain read; the rest load by script. In a headless browser (no sign-in) I scrolled and pressed "load more" until the page stopped growing (about 71,000 characters of text) and searched the text for the three clouds. I did not confirm that the list was complete, so a miss is not proof of absence. Roles only, no names (people only by public professional role):
- **Amazon:** one speaker listed as a vice president (people, experience and technology) at Amazon, United States. A second entry mentions Amazon only as a former union presidency, which is not a company speaker.
- **Microsoft:** one speaker listed as a global director of creative at Microsoft, United States.
- **AWS, Amazon Web Services, Google, Google Cloud, Azure:** no match in the loaded text.
So on the speakers page as loaded, the three centres of gravity are thin: Amazon and Microsoft each have one corporate speaker (neither from a cloud division), Google and AWS none. This matches the earlier rounds (thin on the 2026 pages; past partners and one Amazon press conference). Method for whoever repeats it: the same page, `scrollTo` and the load-more control; the count of entries should be taken first so completeness is known.

Completeness check, read 7 November 2026 (about 07:20 UTC): the speakers page says "Learn from 800 speakers"; the scrolled page held 941 distinct links to speaker or attendee profiles (the count includes some non-speaker links, so it is an upper figure). The list therefore looks fully loaded, and the finding stands for the page as published at the time of reading: Amazon one corporate speaker, Microsoft one, Google and AWS none. The page will change as more speakers are announced; repeat it before the summit.
