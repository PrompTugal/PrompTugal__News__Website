## Round 7, read 7 November 2026 (about 20:25 UTC): Microsoft's announcement at Web Summit 2025, from the trade agency's own magazine note
Source: AICEP's [Portugalglobal magazine note "Web Summit: the meeting point for startups and AI"](https://portugalglobal.pt/en/news/2025/december/web-summit-the-meeting-point-for-startups-and-ai), dated 11 December 2025 (a public agency; read in full). It says that at the opening session of the tenth Web Summit Paddy Cosgrave put AI at the centre, with US tariffs and China's technology as the other themes, and that "one of the most significant announcements came from Microsoft, which revealed a ten billion dollar investment in a data centre in Sines, in partnership with Start Campus and Nscale", presented as positioning Portugal as a strategic AI hub in the EU. So **Microsoft made its largest Portugal announcement in Web Summit week 2025** (the week of 10 to 13 November 2025), even though Microsoft is not on the 2026 partners list and has one corporate speaker in 2026 (rounds 4 and 5): the centre of gravity can sit in the country's infrastructure news and not in the summit's own lists. The agency's note is a summary of the magazine's issue; the announcement's own wording from Microsoft is **not** read, and a Euronews report on it refused a plain fetch (HTTP 406), so I did not use it.
The same note records two Portuguese outcomes of the 2025 edition: the winner of Road 2 Web Summit (a fertility-technology startup) and the winner of the Web Summit pitch competition (a fintech startup), named in the magazine; only the roles are recorded here.
Next: Microsoft's own announcement (news.microsoft.com or its Portugal site) for the exact wording and the partners (Start Campus, Nscale); a 2026 follow-up to see whether Microsoft is on the programme.

## Round 8, read 7 November 2026 (about 21:25 UTC): Nscale's own releases on Microsoft in Sines
Microsoft's own announcement was not found (a search restricted to Microsoft's and the partners' sites returned only Nscale's pages). Two press releases by Nscale (the AI-infrastructure company named in the AICEP note) were read in full at source:
- **15 October 2025** ([Nscale contracts approximately 200,000 NVIDIA GB300 GPUs with Microsoft](https://www.nscale.com/press-releases/nscale-microsoft-2025)): "From Q1 2026, Nscale will deliver approximately 12,600 NVIDIA GB300 GPUs at the Start Campus data centre in Sines, Portugal", a multi-year agreement to deliver NVIDIA AI infrastructure services to Microsoft and "sovereign AI solutions within the EU" for European customers; one of four countries in the deal (Norway, the UK, the US and Portugal). So the Microsoft-Sines link predates Web Summit 2025 by a month.
- **5 May 2026** ([Nscale to deliver 66,000+ NVIDIA Rubin GPUs to Microsoft at Start Campus' site in Portugal](https://www.nscale.com/press-releases/nscale-start-campus)): an expansion of the collaboration with Microsoft and Start Campus; Nscale's additional investments of EUR 230 million in shared infrastructure and EUR 465 million in a second 200 MW building at the SINES Data Campus; the release itself calls it "one of the largest AI infrastructure investments in Portugal's history"; Start Campus "fully permitted for 1.2GW".
**What is and is not confirmed:** both releases are by Nscale and describe Nscale's deliveries to Microsoft and its own investments. Neither states a "ten billion dollar" Microsoft investment in the parts read; that figure appears in AICEP's December 2025 note (round 7), which I read but could not check at Microsoft. So: a Microsoft-Nscale-Start Campus build in Sines is confirmed by Nscale; the USD 10 billion figure is **AICEP's report of a Microsoft announcement, unconfirmed at the source**. Do not publish the figure as Microsoft's without Microsoft's own page or a second primary source.

## Round 9, read 7 November 2026 (about 22:25 UTC): Start Campus's own release confirms the figure and the Web Summit link
Source: Start Campus's press release [Microsoft makes one of its largest investments in Europe at Start Campus in Portugal](https://www.startcampus.pt/microsoft-makes-one-of-its-largest-investments-in-europe-at-start-campus-in-portugal), dated **Lisbon, 11 November 2025** (English and Portuguese on the same page; the press-contact lines are deliberately not recorded). Read in full. It says: "Following remarks made by Brad Smith, Vice Chair and President of Microsoft, during the opening of the Web Summit in Lisbon, Start Campus welcomes Microsoft's announcement to invest $10 billion in the company's SINES data center campus." It adds that the investment "results from strategic discussions between Microsoft, Nscale, and Start Campus", covering the operational SIN01 facility and the next phase of the campus, with details to be announced "in coordination with Microsoft and Nscale in due course". It describes the campus as 1.2 GW, a total combined investment value estimated at EUR 8.5 billion, 100% renewable energy.
- **What this settles:** the USD 10 billion figure and its venue are now confirmed by a second primary source (the campus operator), not only AICEP's summary (round 7): Microsoft's president announced it in remarks at the **opening of Web Summit Lisbon on 10 November 2025**, and Start Campus issued its release on 11 November 2025. Wording rule: say "Microsoft announced, in remarks at the opening of Web Summit 2025, an investment of USD 10 billion in the Sines data centre campus, as reported by the campus operator Start Campus"; the amount is Start Campus's report of Microsoft's announcement, and **Microsoft's own page is still not read**.
- **What it changes for round 8:** the Nscale releases (October 2025 and May 2026) describe the deliveries that followed; the EUR 230 million and EUR 465 million are Nscale's own spending, a different thing from Microsoft's USD 10 billion.
- **Centre-of-gravity reading:** Microsoft is absent from the organiser's 2026 partners list yet used the 2025 opening to make its largest Portugal announcement; the relation is "announced at the summit's opening", which is neither partner nor exhibitor nor speaker's-employer in the lists. Whether Microsoft appears at the 2026 opening is not yet known.

## Round 10, read 7 November 2026 (about 23:25 UTC): a discrepancy to carry into the wording, and the 2026 schedule
- **The organiser's own 2025 Opening Night page** ([session page](https://websummit.com/sessions/lis25/e3f8fdfa-0d57-4b56-bea3-988495e3c8a8/opening-night/), 10 November 2025, 6 to 8 PM, Centre Stage) lists these speakers: the chief executive of Lovable, a tennis legend, a content creator, an IBM chief corporate affairs and brand officer, Visa's global head of product (CMS), the editors-in-chief of Newsweek and TechCrunch, TIME's chief executive, Web Summit's chief executive and founder, the Mayor of Lisbon, and Portugal's minister for state reform. **Microsoft's president is not on that page.** Start Campus's release (round 9) says his remarks were made "during the opening of the Web Summit in Lisbon". The two can both be true (a remark from the stage or a segment the listing does not name), but I cannot reconcile them from public pages, and I did not read a transcript or video.
- **Wording rule, tightened:** say "Microsoft's announcement of USD 10 billion for the Sines data centre campus was reported by Start Campus on 11 November 2025, after remarks by Microsoft's president at the opening of Web Summit Lisbon"; do not say he was a listed speaker, and do not give a stage or time.

Session pages exist at `/sessions/lis26/<id>/<slug>/`. In the part read there is no Microsoft, AWS, Amazon or Google session; the 2026 Opening Night line-up is **not found** (the 2026 schedule shows 9 to 12 November on its day tabs). Search by session pages is the route if a name is needed.
