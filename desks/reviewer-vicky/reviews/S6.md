# S6 review: Gender in Tech report: 84% see women in leadership

**Verdict: Fix first.** Every figure and quote checks out. Two small fixes for the journalist. At 520 words it needs no cut.

Source opened on 5 November 2026 in a browser: Web Summit's article of 27 October. Also read: Web Summit's women in tech page (<https://websummit.com/women-in-tech/>), for point 3.

## Fix first

1. **Front matter has no `beat`.** The curator's map files S6 under Announcements. Brief 3 also lists `license` and `tags`.
2. **"She said Web Summit would keep using its platform ..."** The source quotes Carolyn Quinlan but does not say which pronoun they use. Repeat the name ("Quinlan said").

## Smaller points

3. **The number of respondents is public.** "What this does not claim" says Web Summit "does not give the number of respondents". The article does not, but Web Summit's women in tech page does: "Ahead of Web Summit 2026, we surveyed more than 1,000 women across our global tech community". Worth adding, with that page as a source and its read date.
4. **DocSend.** From this session the report link returned a 403 (blocked), not an email form. Either way it was not read. "Could not be read" covers both.
5. **"That is the headline finding."** Web Summit lists "Women are leading" fifth of five findings. It is the first half of the article's headline, though ("Women are leading in tech. So why are so many considering leaving?"). "That is the first line of the report's headline" is closer.
6. **Promptugal's angle.** A suggestion: "Women at the summit could look for Web Summit's women in tech community, whose 2026 programme is sold out, and whose members say the summit is where they met collaborators." Only with the women in tech page as its source.

## Checked and correct

Seventh annual report, published 27 October 2026, before Lisbon (9 to 12 November); 84% and 35%; half experience sexism, 58% not paid fairly; 42%, 52% for women aged 35 to 44, 56% know a woman who has scaled back or left; 75% last year, 31% this year, 34% no impact, 16% cannot tell; 58% on content romanticising homemaking, and burnout; Carolyn Quinlan's quotes, exact; more than 70,000 attendees from over 100 countries. "What this does not claim" is fair and clear.

Tone and safety: positive and fair, without hiding the hard findings; one person named, in their public role; no contact details; nothing on RiskMandate.

## Length

520 words from "In short" to the end of "What this does not claim": within 400 to 600, no cut needed. The style pass will only tidy, once the fixes are in.
