**Verdict: Ready.** Approved at Vicky's standing authorisation of 10 November, and `status: approved` recorded. draft_lint gave 0 errors, and publish_check passes. Published as `content/articles/five-coding-agents-reverts-security-waits.md`.

## Checked at the source (arXiv:2609.17598, abstract page, read 24 November 2026)

- **The paper:** title and author as given, "[v1] Sat, 12 Sep 2026". No journal reference on the page, so the article's "does not say whether the paper has been peer reviewed" holds.
- **The sample:** "37,623 provenance-labeled pull requests (PRs) from five commercial agents (OpenAI Codex, Devin, GitHub Copilot, Cursor, and Claude Code) and a matched human baseline, drawn from 2,807 GitHub repositories between December 2024 and July 2025".
- **Reverts:** "quality differences are vendor-specific rather than uniform: Codex-authored PRs were reverted about half as often as human PRs (6.1% vs. 11.5% ...), while Devin PRs were reverted more often (14.5% ...)".
- **Security:** "agent code pooled across vendors was less likely than human code to contain a security smell ... driven by fewer hardcoded credentials and eval-style constructs".
- **Review:** "Copilot PRs drew the most human reviews and change requests, and Claude Code PRs waited the longest for a first human review (median 12.6 hours)".

Each finding is attributed to the paper. The article says what was read (the abstract only) and what the paper measured (reverts, not outages).
