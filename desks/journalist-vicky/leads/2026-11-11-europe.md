## The Commission preliminarily finds TikTok's "For You" feed in breach of the DSA over "rabbit holes"
- source: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-preliminarily-finds-tiktoks-you-feed-breach-digital-services-act-harmful-rabbit-holes

- checked: 2026-11-11 06:30 UTC
- new: the Commission has preliminarily found TikTok in breach of the Digital Services Act over the "rabbit hole effect" of its recommender system: the more a user interacts with a type of content, the more the "For You" feed recommends similar content, which the Commission says can harm users' mental health, especially vulnerable adults and minors. The findings cover both risk assessment and mitigation measures.

## Follow-up for the curator's request (TikTok): the full press release, and TikTok's response not found
- source: https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/ip_26_2361
- published: 6 November 2026 (IP/26/2361)
- checked: 2026-11-11 07:25 UTC
- new: the full press release gives the Commission's reasoning and next steps. Risk assessment: TikTok "did not adequately assess" how its algorithm may lead users into "rabbit holes"; the algorithm reads watching and liking as signs of interest and recommends more of the same. Mitigation: TikTok's existing measures are found "not effective" (the "not interested" button "may not be enough"; the algorithm prioritises viewing time over users' choice to see less). At this stage the Commission considers TikTok "needs to change the design" of the feed, for example relying more on explicit preferences, disabling viewing time as a recommendation signal, improving user controls and testing its algorithm regularly. Next steps: TikTok may examine the file and reply in writing; the European Board for Digital Services is consulted; if confirmed, a non-compliance decision "can trigger a fine" of up to 6% of total worldwide annual turnover. Background: part of formal DSA proceedings that also produced preliminary findings on addictive design (February 2026), protection of minors (July 2026) and researchers' data access (October 2025); advertising transparency was closed with binding commitments (December 2025). Ireland's Coimisiún na Meán supported the investigation.
- establishes: the Commission's preliminary view, its stated evidence base (risk assessment reports, internal data, replies to requests for information, research, expert interviews) and the procedure. "These preliminary findings do not prejudge the outcome of the investigation." No fine has been imposed.
- TikTok's public response: not found. TikTok's newsroom (newsroom.tiktok.com, the EU, UK and global editions, checked 07:28 UTC) shows no post on these findings on its front pages. A response quoted by the press, if any, was not looked for (the curator asked for TikTok's own page). A story should say TikTok's view is not known from its own channels.

## The Commission proposes a Digital Fairness Act, including rights when an AI agent buys for you
- source: https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/ip_26_2407
- published: the press release's own date is 11 November 2026 (IP/26/2407); the press corner's feed lists it under Tuesday 10 November. A Q&A accompanies it: https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/qanda_26_2408 (not opened in this scan)
- checked: 2026-11-11 10:25 UTC
- new: the Commission presented its proposal for a Digital Fairness Act (DFA), a major update of EU consumer law for online shopping, with a revision of the Consumer Protection Cooperation Regulation giving the Commission direct powers to investigate and fine in large-scale cross-border cases, and a simplification directive amending the Consumer Rights Directive. Key points it lists: a ban on manipulative "dark patterns"; clear pricing for in-app virtual currencies and dynamic pricing for cultural and sports events; an option to turn off addictive design features; a ban on gambling-like features (pay-to-win, pay-to-progress) for minors; influencer disclosure; limits on personalised ads and prices targeting minors or using sensitive data; easier cancellation and an end to subscription traps; a right to talk to a human in customer service; and "clear rights when using an AI agent to buy products on their behalf". The Commission expects savings for consumers of "up to €13.7 billion a year".
- establishes: what the Commission proposes and expects, in its own words. It is a proposal: the European Parliament and the Council must still agree it, and nothing here applies yet. The AI-agent rights are named but not detailed in the release (the Q&A may say more).

- web_summit_confirmation: n/a
- desk: europe (fits the agentic theme: the first EU proposal we have seen that names consumers' rights when an AI agent shops for them; nothing on the DFA in content/ or the drafts, checked 10:25 UTC)
