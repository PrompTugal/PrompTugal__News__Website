# Road 2 Web Summit: Portugal's startups head for Lisbon

## In short

- Road 2 Web Summit, run by Startup Portugal with Web Summit, is in its 11th edition.
- It selects up to 125 Portuguese startups and scaleups for a bootcamp in Porto and Lisbon.
- Those selected get 50% off Web Summit's ALPHA or BETA package and extra exhibition space on the Portugal stand.
- Galp sponsors two cash prizes: 15,000 euros for the most promising startup and 5,000 euros for the best performance.
- Three Portuguese startups from the last five editions have won Web Summit's PITCH competition: Smartex, Intuitivo and Granter.

## The story

Portugal sends its startups to Web Summit with a plan. Road 2 Web Summit is a programme designed by Startup Portugal with Web Summit. It helps startups based in Portugal prepare for the event, according to Startup Portugal.

The 2026 edition is the 11th. Applications opened in August, Tek Notícias reported. The programme selects up to 125 Portuguese companies for an intensive bootcamp, with sessions in Porto and Lisbon. Investors, founders, pitch trainers and people from Web Summit take part.

Being selected brings practical help. Startups get 50% off Web Summit's ALPHA or BETA package, and extra exhibition space on Portugal's stand, among other benefits.

Galp sponsors the programme again. It funds two prizes: 15,000 euros for the Most Promising Startup and 5,000 euros for the Best Performing Startup. Last year they went to Enhanced Fertility, in health, and Teemy, in hospitality.

The programme has a record. Tek Notícias reports that it has supported more than 1,200 Portuguese startups since 2016. In 2025 alone it supported 115 startups, which raised 40 million euros. Startup Portugal's own page says it has helped more than 1,000 startups over more than ten years.

The stage has paid off, too. In the last five editions, three Portuguese startups won Web Summit's PITCH competition: Smartex, Intuitivo and, most recently, Granter.

At the summit, the Portugal stand is the place to find this year's class. Investors looking at Portuguese early-stage companies could start there.

## What this does not claim

- **This year's class is not named.** Neither source lists the 2026 startups or how many were finally chosen. Up to 125 is the limit, not the number.
- **The totals differ.** Tek Notícias says more than 1,200 startups since 2016; Startup Portugal's page says more than 1,000. Both are given as reported.
- **The 2025 figures** (115 startups, 40 million euros raised) come from Tek Notícias. The newsroom has not seen Startup Portugal's own figures for them.
- **The bootcamp dates and the prize winners for 2026** are not known from these sources.
- The stand's location in the venue is not given.
