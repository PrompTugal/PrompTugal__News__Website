> **Update, 20 November 2026.** The [gazette's standard terms](https://files.diariodarepublica.pt/gratuitos/2s/2026/02/2S037A0000S00.pdf) of 23 February 2026 hold more rules than this article gave. The gazette says:
>
> - **Patients.** They may choose their provider freely, and the price conditions for them are those set in the convention, with no others demanded. Care is given at the address the patient names, on an electronic prescription from their health service doctor.
> - **Joining.** A provider must submit the device's EU conformity declaration, a statement that it meets EU medical-device rules, and its class IIa registration certificate. A missing or non-compliant document means immediate, automatic exclusion, with no chance to fix it.
> - **Oversight.** The health system's central administration may audit the care billed and the platform, including its records and logs. It may ask for statistics on users, response times and functional gains.
> - **Data.** The platform must work fully with the health service's systems, such as the electronic health record and prescriptions. The provider may not prevent data portability, which means moving a patient's data when the convention ends.
> - **Ending.** Either side may end the convention by email on 60 days' notice, after the first six months. The administration may end it for a serious breach.
>
> These are the rules as published in February. We found no source on whether any provider has joined or how the rules have been applied.
