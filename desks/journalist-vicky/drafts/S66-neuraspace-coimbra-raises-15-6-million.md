## In short

- Neuraspace, based in Coimbra, has secured 15.6 million euros, according to AICEP, Portugal's trade and investment agency.
- Lince Capital, Explorer Investments and Armilar Venture Partners invest, together with public money from Portugal's Recovery and Resilience Plan.
- The money goes to its AI platform, its own optical sensors and a version of its products for defence customers.
- The company's own site says a human operator chooses what a satellite does.

## The story

A Coimbra company that builds AI software for satellites has raised new money.

AICEP reported on 3 September 2026 that Neuraspace has secured 15.6 million euros. Tech.eu had reported the same round on 5 August. The funding combines private investment with money from Portugal's Recovery and Resilience Plan, the country's programme for spending EU recovery funds.

Neuraspace says it will spend the money on four things. It will expand its AI platform and speed up what it calls autonomous mission operations. It will add to its own optical sensors, which watch objects in orbit. And it will develop NeuraspaceDEF, a dual-use product, meaning one for both civil and military customers, aimed at governments and defence buyers.

The company works on space domain awareness, which means knowing what is in orbit and what it is doing, and space traffic management, which means keeping satellites from colliding. AICEP and Tech.eu say its platform combines data from commercial and government sensors with AI that assesses risk and supports decisions. AICEP says space is getting more crowded and that satellites also face cyberattacks, radio interference and GNSS spoofing and jamming, which is faking or blocking satellite navigation signals.

Who decides? The company's home page describes the routine. Operators receive warnings of close approaches between objects in orbit. Neuraspace says its software rates the risk and suggests manoeuvres up to five days ahead. One operator can then review a high-risk case and pick from a short list of suggestions within minutes. The company says a team without such tools typically needs about eight hours.

AICEP gives these figures about the company:

- It was founded in 2020.
- Its revenue grew by more than 350 percent in the past year.
- It monitors more than 600 satellites for commercial and institutional customers, the European Space Agency among them.
- It has contracts with the Portuguese Air Force and NATO.
- A European ministry of defence recently chose it as prime contractor.

AICEP adds that the platform contributes to Europe's technological sovereignty. Armilar, one of the three investors, also led an earlier round of 2.5 million euros, its own post of 23 March 2022 says.

Satellite operators and investors in European space technology are the natural people to talk to Neuraspace.

## What this does not claim

- **The figures are the company's.** AICEP and Tech.eu appear to draw on the same announcement. Neither the round nor the customer claims were checked with Neuraspace or the investors.
- **The split is not given.** No source says how much comes from each investor or from the Recovery and Resilience Plan.
- **No results are given for the software.** The only accuracy figure the newsroom found is a claim of 22 percent fewer false alarms, in Armilar's 2022 post about an earlier version. It is Neuraspace's claim as the investor relays it, and it is unchecked.
- **How far the system acts alone is not clear.** AICEP and Tech.eu say "autonomous decision support". They do not say when a person must approve.
- **The defence customer is not named,** and no contract value or date is given.
