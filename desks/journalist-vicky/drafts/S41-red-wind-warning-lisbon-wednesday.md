## In short

- [IPMA](../organisations/ipma/index.html), Portugal's weather institute, has a red wind warning for the Lisbon district on Wednesday 11 November, from 12:00 to 21:00.
- It expects gusts up to 120 km/h, and up to 140 km/h in the most exposed places.
- Orange warnings cover heavy rain (05:00 to 18:00) and rough seas (12:00 to 21:00) on the same day.
- Portugal's government has declared an [alert situation](https://portugal.gov.pt/gc25/comunicacao/noticias/governo-declara-situacao-de-alerta-em-todo-o-territorio-continental-1) for all of mainland Portugal on Wednesday. Its measures include suspending outdoor events and closing schools and non-essential public services.
- As of 17:32 UTC on Tuesday, no change to Wednesday's programme had been announced on [Web Summit](../organisations/web-summit/index.html)'s official channels we checked: its homepage, blog and news pages.

## The story

Wednesday is the third day of [Web Summit Lisbon 2026](../events/web-summit-lisbon-2026/index.html). IPMA's open data, read at 14:23 UTC on Tuesday 10 November and unchanged at 17:32 UTC on Tuesday, shows a red warning for wind in the Lisbon district that day. Red is IPMA's highest level. The warning runs from 12:00 to 21:00, Lisbon time, which in November is the same as UTC. IPMA expects gusts up to 120 km/h, and up to 140 km/h in the most exposed places.

The same data shows an orange warning for rain from 05:00 to 18:00 on Wednesday, described as at times heavy and persistent. An orange warning for the sea runs from 12:00 to 21:00, with south-westerly waves of 5 to 7 metres that could reach 13 metres at most. Tuesday already had yellow warnings for rain, thunder and the sea.

IPMA's daily forecast for Lisbon, updated at 13:31 on Tuesday, gives a 100% chance of rain for every day from Tuesday to Friday. It forecasts highs of 19.6 to 21.1 C.

Portugal's government has [declared](https://portugal.gov.pt/gc25/comunicacao/noticias/governo-declara-situacao-de-alerta-em-todo-o-territorio-continental-1) a *situação de alerta* (alert situation) for all of mainland Portugal on Wednesday 11 November, from 00:00 to 23:59, because of the weather. Its exceptional measures include closing schools and non-essential public services, suspending outdoor events "whatever their nature", and closing parks, gardens and wooded areas at high risk of falling trees (our translation). Citing IPMA, the declaration says gusts could exceed 150 km/h if a "sting jet" forms. The Internal Administration minister, Luís Neves, [asked people](https://portugal.gov.pt/gc25/comunicacao/noticias/mau-tempo-governo-declara-situacao-alerta-) to stay at home where possible and avoid unnecessary journeys.

Portugal's civil protection authority, ANEPC, has [raised its special readiness state](https://prociv.gov.pt/pt/noticias/depressao-charles-anepc-avisa-populacao-por-sms/) and sent an SMS warning to the population about the strong wind and persistent rain on Wednesday.

We checked Web Summit's own channels at 14:23 and again at 17:32 UTC on Tuesday. As of 17:32 UTC, no change to Wednesday's programme had been announced on them: its homepage and its blog and news pages had no notice about the weather, the warning or the programme. Its support centre and FAQ pages refused our automated reader, so we could not check them. If Web Summit announces anything, its own channels are where to look first.

IPMA updates its warnings during the day. The levels and times here are as read at 17:32 UTC on Tuesday; check IPMA for the latest.

Anyone at the summit can follow IPMA's warnings for the Lisbon district and ANEPC's notices at the source, alongside Web Summit's own channels.
