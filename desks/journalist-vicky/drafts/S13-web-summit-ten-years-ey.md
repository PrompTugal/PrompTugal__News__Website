# Web Summit's ten Lisbon years: 2.5 billion euros, says EY

## In short

- An EY report says Web Summit generated about 250 million euros a year for Portugal's economy, almost 2.5 billion euros over ten editions.
- After the cost of hosting it, Portugal gains more than 230 million euros a year, the report says.
- Over ten editions more than 683,000 people came and spent 730 million euros on hotels, food, transport and shopping.
- The number of startups in Portugal grew 132%, from 2,193 in 2016 to 5,091 in 2025, according to the report.

## The story

Ten editions on, a report puts a number on what Web Summit has meant for Portugal. The consultancy EY's report was presented at Web Summit's press conference before the event, on 5 November, Executive Digest reported.

The total impact is 2,492 million euros, according to the report. It comes from visitors' spending, foreign investment and the growth of Portuguese startups. That is about 250 million euros a year. After the cost of hosting the event, the country gains more than 230 million euros a year.

Visitors are a large part of it. More than 683,000 people attended over ten editions. They spent 730 million euros on accommodation, restaurants, transport and shops, with a peak of 118 million euros in 2025. The activity linked to the event supports more than 3,000 full-time jobs, the report says.

Taxes paid in Portugal because of Web Summit rose from 25 million euros in 2016 to 50.7 million euros in 2025. That includes 4.7 million euros from the company's own presence in Lisbon, according to EY.

The startup figures are the most striking. Portugal had 2,193 startups in 2016 and 5,091 in 2025, a rise of 132%. At the end of last year they employed the equivalent of 28,000 full-time workers. Startups took part in the event 100,000 times over ten editions. The 191 companies in the 2024 startup programme alone raised 607.5 million euros between them.

The report also counts reputation. Between 2016 and 2026 the event drew about 114,000 mentions in international media, worth 3 billion dollars in advertising value, it says.

Paddy Cosgrave, Web Summit's chief executive, said there will be more Portuguese startups this year than last, and about 70,000 visitors. On whether the event stays in Portugal, he said talks continue and several European cities are interested.

Investors at the summit who want to see what the report counts can start with this year's Portuguese startups, on the Portugal stand and in the startup programme.

## What this does not claim

- **The figures are EY's**, in a report presented by Web Summit, as reported by Executive Digest. The newsroom has not read the report itself and has not checked its method.
- **"Generated" is the report's word.** The sources do not say how EY separates the event's effect from other causes, for example in the growth of startups.
- **The event's future in Lisbon is not known.** Paddy Cosgrave said only that negotiations continue.
- The press conference also covered recent protests in Lisbon. That is not part of this economic story and is not covered here.
