## In short

- Eleven startups on the first page of Web Summit's featured list are filed under healthtech and wellness. That page is 1 of 26, and all 100 startups on it are at the GROWTH stage, so the eleven are not a complete or representative list.
- Four help people get care: a doctor by chat, telemedicine under a partner's brand, an AI assistant for clinics, and claims for insurers.
- Three help find out what is wrong sooner: a blood test at the pharmacy, cognitive checks from speech, and faster diagnosis of rare diseases.
- Two work on new treatments, and two on recovery and wellbeing.
- Each description is the company's own, from its page on Web Summit's site. We suggest no meetings.

## The story

The eleven health and wellness startups on the first page of Web Summit's featured list tackle very different problems. Here they are, grouped by the problem each describes.

### Getting care

**[Olá Doutor](../companies/ola-doutor/index.html)** is an app that connects patients with licensed doctors through instant chat, with no appointment needed. Patients can get guidance, prescriptions and follow-up from their phone.

**[Rapidoc](../companies/rapidoc/index.html)** lets partners launch telemedicine under their own brand. It provides the technology, 24-hour medical services and medical teams, and says it operates in Brazil, the US and Angola.

**SPRYT** builds Asa, an AI agent for healthcare providers. It talks to patients over WhatsApp and SMS about bookings, reminders and payments, to cut missed appointments.

**Nice Deer** connects insurers, providers and patients in one system, handling claims, fraud checks and payments across the Middle East and North Africa.

### Finding out what is wrong, sooner

**Igloo** brings blood tests to the pharmacy counter. One finger-prick checks ten markers, such as diabetes, thyroid and vitamin levels, with results in about 15 minutes. It says it is live in 300 pharmacies in 10 countries.

**[Mobile Brain](../companies/mobile-brain/index.html)** turns spontaneous speech into measures of cognitive and emotional health, for early screening in schools, healthcare and companies.

**[Raras Health](../companies/raras-health/index.html)** uses AI and genomics to shorten the wait for a rare-disease diagnosis, from years to days, it says. It confirms diagnoses in its own lab and follows patients through treatment.

### New ways to treat

**AURIMOD**, based in Vienna, makes VIVO, a wearable that sits behind the ear. The company says it offers a drug-free way to treat chronic pain. It says the device is CE-marked and reimbursed in Austria.

**NeoCannBio**, a Korean biopharma company, says it is building a full chain for medical cannabinoids, from cultivation to finished products, and is developing new cannabinoid drugs for rare diseases.

### Recovery and wellbeing

**Seroton** combines sound, touch and body sensors with AI to help people relax deeply and recover, in furniture for homes and rehabilitation centres.

**[sheerME](../companies/sheerme/index.html)** is an app for finding, booking and paying for beauty and wellness services.

**Who could connect:** anyone working on access to care, diagnosis or recovery could look these companies up on Web Summit's featured list. This is a suggestion only.

- **The groups are ours.** Some companies could fit more than one.
- **We do not know when or where each startup exhibits.** The list gives no days or stands.
