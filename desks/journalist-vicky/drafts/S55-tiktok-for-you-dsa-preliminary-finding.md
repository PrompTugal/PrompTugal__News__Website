## In short

- On 6 November 2026, the European Commission preliminarily found [TikTok](../organisations/tiktok/index.html) in breach of the Digital Services Act (DSA) over the "rabbit hole" effect of its For You feed.
- The Commission says TikTok did not adequately assess the risk, and that its existing safeguards are not effective.
- At this stage, it considers TikTok needs to change the feed's design, for example by not using viewing time to recommend content.
- This is not a final decision. TikTok can reply in writing. If the finding is confirmed, a fine of up to 6% of worldwide annual turnover is possible.
- We found no public response from TikTok on its own newsroom.

## The story

The [European Commission](../companies/european-commission/index.html) has told TikTok, in a preliminary view, that its For You feed breaks the EU's platform law. The concern is what the Commission calls the "rabbit hole" effect. The more a user watches or likes a kind of video, the more of the same the feed recommends. That can be harmful when the videos are about sadness, isolation or body image, the Commission says, especially for minors and vulnerable adults.

The finding has two parts. First, the Commission says TikTok "did not adequately assess" how its algorithm may lead users into these rabbit holes. The algorithm reads watching and liking as signs of interest. Its example: if someone keeps watching videos about weight loss, the feed shows them more. Second, it says TikTok's existing measures are "not effective". Efforts to diversify the feed do not cut repeated content enough. The "not interested" button may not be enough to change what a user sees. The algorithm puts viewing time ahead of a user's choice to see less of something.

At this stage, the Commission considers that TikTok "needs to change the design" of the For You feed. Its examples: rely more on what users say they want, stop using viewing time as a signal for recommendations, make user controls easier to find and use, and test the algorithm regularly.

What happens next is set out in the release. TikTok can read the Commission's investigation file and reply in writing. The European Board for Digital Services will be consulted. If the Commission's view is confirmed, it may adopt a non-compliance decision. That can lead to a fine of up to 6% of TikTok's total worldwide annual turnover. The Commission adds that these findings "do not prejudge the outcome of the investigation".

This is one strand of a wider case. Within the same proceedings, the Commission reached preliminary findings on addictive design in February 2026, on the protection of minors in July 2026 and on researchers' access to public data in October 2025. A strand on advertising transparency closed in December 2025, when TikTok made binding commitments. Ireland's digital services regulator, Coimisiún na Meán, supported the investigation.

## What this does not claim

- **A preliminary view only.** The Commission has not decided that TikTok broke the law, and no fine has been imposed.
- **TikTok's side.** We found no post on these findings on TikTok's newsroom (EU, UK and global editions, checked 11 November). TikTok may have commented elsewhere; we have not quoted any response.
- **The evidence.** The Commission says it used TikTok's risk assessments, internal data and documents, research and expert interviews. We have not seen them.
