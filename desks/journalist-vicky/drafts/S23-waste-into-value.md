## In short

- Two Portuguese startups are turning waste streams into products: pineapple by-products into fish feed, and desalination brine into freshwater and minerals.
- [AgroGrINTech](../organisations/agrogrintech/index.html) runs a pilot unit in Maia that can make about 200kg a year of bromelain, an enzyme, from around 120 tonnes of pineapple by-products.
- [Seamoretech](../organisations/seamoretech/index.html), from Porto, has raised €1.1 million to bring its brine technology to market, with a launch in Spain planned for 2027.
- Both grew from university research: AgroGrINTech's process from the [Portuguese Catholic University](../organisations/portuguese-catholic-university/index.html), Seamoretech as a spin-off of the [University of Porto](../organisations/university-of-porto/index.html)'s engineering faculty.
- Linking them is the newsroom's suggestion. No source says they work together.

## The story

Waste is the raw material for two young Portuguese companies.

### Pineapple into fish feed

AgroGrINTech takes the peel and core of pineapples and extracts bromelain, an enzyme. The bromelain is used to make protein ingredients for feed for farmed sea bass and sea bream, AICEP Portugal Global reports. The process also yields pineapple flour.

The pilot unit in Maia has a stated capacity of about 200kg of bromelain a year. The company says that is four times the 50kg it forecast for 2025.

The work is part of Pep4Fish, a project led by the [ETSA Group](../organisations/etsa/index.html) with nine business and research partners. It is expected to secure about €1.2 million by the end of 2026, from private money and Portugal's Recovery and Resilience Plan. The process builds on research at the Catholic University's Centre for Biotechnology and Fine Chemistry, licensed to AgroGrINTech in 2025. The company plans its first supply contracts in 2027.

### Brine into water and minerals

Desalination leaves behind brine, a highly concentrated waste. Seamoretech, founded in 2021 as a spin-off of the University of Porto's Faculty of Engineering, recovers purified freshwater and minerals from it, using forward osmosis in container units. The company says it can recover magnesium, calcium, copper and lithium, and aims to leave no liquid waste.

It has raised €1.1 million in the first closing of its seed round, from Rypples, Seven Seas Capital and Crédito Agrícola, Portugal Startup News reports. That brings its total to €3 million. It plans to launch in Spain in 2027 and is lining up pilots in Spain and Egypt.

### What they share

Different industries, the same idea: what one process throws away becomes another's input. Both started from Portuguese university labs, and both are moving from pilot to sale.

**Who could connect:** investors and corporates looking for circular-economy startups from Portugal could look at both, and at the universities behind them. This is a suggestion only.
