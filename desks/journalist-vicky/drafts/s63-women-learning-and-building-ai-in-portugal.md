## In short

- Several free programmes now aim to get more women into AI in Portugal: an online course through the APDC, a certified course for 500 women, a one-day build session and a leadership forum.
- Portuguese Women in Tech, founded in 2016, runs mentoring and entrepreneurship programmes for female founders.
- Portuguese press reports that 14% of Portuguese startups are founded or led by women, and that few women say they want to start a tech business.
- Most of these programmes are about learning and building with AI. They are not a list of women who have built AI companies in Portugal.

PME Magazine puts the starting point plainly: 14% of Portuguese startups are founded or led by women. It also reports a Mastercard study of March 2025 in which 57% of women in Portugal described themselves as entrepreneurs, but only 4% named technology and IT as the field they would start a business in, against 15% of men. Here is what is on offer.

**A free online course.** Human Resources reported on 12 March 2026 that the APDC, Portugal's digital association, had joined Microsoft Portugal and Founderz to bring AI Skills 4 Women to Portugal. It is an online programme, free of charge, meant to build digital skills and bring more women closer to AI opportunities.

**A certified course for 500 women.** In September 2025 Executiva reported that BORA Mulheres, a free entrepreneurship programme run by Coca-Cola Europacific Partners and Impact Hub Lisbon, was launching its first Academia, on AI. The certified online course ran from October to November 2025 in four weekly modules, covering AI in everyday use, ethics, critical thinking and job-market trends, in Portuguese with Portuguese Sign Language interpretation. The programme says it has had seven editions and more than 5,200 entrepreneurs enrolled since 2019.

**A day of building.** On 27 October, the W35 community held Build Lab in Lisbon, Porto and Faro, about 50 participants a city, according to PME Magazine. After a first part on deciding what to build, participants had 90 minutes to build a first prototype with AI tools. Lovable gave each a hundred credits. No coding or AI experience was needed.

**Leadership and the conversation.** On 13 November, in Lisbon, the 2nd International Women in Digital Forum (Fórum Internacional Mulheres no Digital), run by MUDA with Women in Tech Portugal, is debating women's leadership in the age of AI in three panels, free with registration, Human Resources reports. In May, API (Artificial Portugal Intelligence) held Women & Allies Shaping AI in Portugal in Porto, a panel and small-group evening on moving AI from pilots to practice, with speakers from retail analytics, healthcare and HR, its own page says.

**The community behind much of it.** Portuguese Women in Tech says it was founded in April 2016 by Liliana Castro and Inês Santos Silva. Its own page lists mentoring, entrepreneurship programmes for female founders, an awards scheme, a hackathon, and a salary-transparency project.

**One founder with a Lisbon link.** Defined.ai, a company that supplies data for AI, says on its own page that it was founded in 2015 by its CEO Daniela Braga, in Seattle, with a research and development centre in Lisbon. The company is American-founded, so it is a Lisbon link, not a Portuguese company.

## What this does not claim

- **This is not a map of women who have built AI products in Portugal.** Most of the items are training, events and community. This newsroom has not counted women founders, researchers or engineers.
- **Figures from press are as the press reports them.** The 14%, the Mastercard 57%, 4% and 15%, and "about 50 a city" come from PME Magazine, which this newsroom read; the Mastercard study itself was not read.
- **No outcomes are claimed.** No source read here says how many women completed these courses or built something lasting.
- **Dates:** Build Lab and the API event have already taken place, and the 13 November forum is the day this was written; nothing here says how they went.
- **Defined.ai is not shown to be Portuguese.** Its own page says it was founded in Seattle; the Lisbon centre is what links it to Portugal. Whether Ms Braga still leads it is not checked here.
- **Nothing here covers Web Summit's own gender report.**
