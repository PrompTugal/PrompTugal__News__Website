> **Update, 21 November 2026.** This article gave the Parliament's and the Commission's descriptions. On 12 May 2026, the law firm [William Fry](https://www.williamfry.com/knowledge/eu-ai-act-omnibus-deal-reached-postponed-deadlines-watermarking-compromise-and-the-nudificiation-prohibition/) wrote its reading of the provisional agreement of 7 May, before the Act was adopted. William Fry says:
>
> - **Why the delay.** The reason is operational, not political. The standards that providers rely on were not expected to be complete until late 2026, and some Member States were slow to name their national authorities.
> - **Products.** Only machinery leaves the high-risk regime. Medical devices, toys and other product types stay in it. The Commission is to set by delegated act, a rule it adopts itself, how the AI rules fit with those products' own approval procedures, within a period running to August 2027.
> - **Marking.** The duty in Article 50, the Act's rule that AI-generated audio, image, video and text carry a machine-readable mark, starts on 2 December 2026. The code of practice, a voluntary guide on how to comply written under the AI Office, was still a draft in May.
> - **Image and video tools.** Providers and deployers are expected to show controls against misuse, in design and in use.
> - **Registration.** Providers must register high-risk systems in the EU database, the EU's public register of such systems, even if they think their system is exempt.
> - **Sandboxes.** The deadline for national authorities to set up regulatory sandboxes, supervised test spaces for AI, is postponed.
>
> This is a law firm's reading of a provisional text. We have not read the final Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, and the firm's statements about standards and the code of practice are as of May. They may have changed.
