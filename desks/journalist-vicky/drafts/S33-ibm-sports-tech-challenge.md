## In short

- [IBM](../companies/ibm/index.html)'s Sports Tech Startup Challenge, launched at Web Summit Lisbon 2025, ends at [Web Summit Lisbon 2026](../events/web-summit-lisbon-2026/index.html) this week.
- On Tuesday 10 November, six startups present AI products for sport to a panel that includes a judge from FIFA, according to IBM.
- On Wednesday 11 November, finalists compete in an invitation-only prize challenge. Six are confirmed so far.
- The prize is a paid proof of concept with IBM worth up to 100,000 US dollars, according to Web Summit.

## The story

IBM's challenge was built to find growth-stage startups making AI products for the business of sport. It started at Web Summit Lisbon 2025. Through 2026, IBM Ventures, the company's venture arm, held showcases at Web Summit's events in Vancouver and Rio and at other technology events, IBM says.

Lisbon hosts the last round, over two days.

**Tuesday 10 November: the showcase.** IBM says six startups will present. Its post names five:

- Proximity Works, which builds real-time intelligence and fan engagement systems;
- More Rewards, which helps clubs predict fan behaviour and personalise offers;
- Game Intuit, a scouting platform that predicts how players will perform in a team;
- N Tangible, which links physical collectibles to ownership records on a blockchain;
- Valarita Sports, which helps people find local sport and fitness events.

Sameer Patel of FIFA and Jose Chieira of TalentAlert.App are among the judges. IBM says the showcase gives no advantage in the prize challenge.

**Wednesday 11 November: the prize challenge.** It is invitation only. IBM names six confirmed finalists: LingoPal (live translation and dubbing for sport), Fanory (income for creators from their fans), Neuron Systems (AI commentary), LivXP (fan experiences), PressBox (content for leagues and media) and TwelveLabs (video search and analysis). More may be invited after Tuesday.

The judges include Sarah Meron, IBM's Chief Communications and Brand Officer, and Marc Gené, Ferrari Ambassador for Scuderia Ferrari HP, with CNN's Brad Smith moderating, according to IBM.

Interested in AI for sport? Tuesday's showcase is the open part of the challenge, and a chance to meet the founders who are building it.

## What this does not claim

- **The time and stage** of either day: neither IBM's post nor Web Summit's page gives them.
- **The sixth showcase startup:** IBM says six will present but names five.
- **The exact prize.** Web Summit says up to 100,000 US dollars in all: up to 80,000 in funding and up to 20,000 in IBM credits. IBM's post says up to 100,000 dollars for the project, plus up to 20,000 in credits. Both say it depends on IBM's rules and a separate agreement.
- **What each startup does** is as IBM describes it.
