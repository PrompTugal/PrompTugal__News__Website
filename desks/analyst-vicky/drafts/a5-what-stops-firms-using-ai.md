**Analysis.** This piece reads official statistics and explains what they show. Every figure is linked to its source below.

## In short

- In 2025, 60.4% of Portuguese firms that had considered AI but did not use it said the cost seemed too high. Across the EU the figure was 38.4%.
- A lack of skills inside the firm was the most cited reason in both, at 74.4% in Portugal and 70.3% in the EU.
- In 2026 the two reasons drew level in Portugal, cost at 64.7% and skills at 65.6%.

## The story

<!-- Chart: the designer draws it from the chart spec in desks/analyst-vicky/data/a5-what-stops-firms-using-ai.md. It goes here, with its caption under it. -->

Every year, INE, Portugal's national statistics institute, asks companies with ten or more staff whether they use AI. It published its 2026 results on 20 November: 19.6% do. The survey also asks firms that do not use AI, but had considered it, why not. They can give several reasons, so the shares add up to more than 100%.

That group is small. In 2026 it was 13.2% of all firms, up from 12.3% in 2025. Most firms that do not use AI never considered it, and the survey does not ask them why.

Eurostat, the EU's statistics office, publishes the same question for every member state. Its latest figures are for 2025. On most reasons Portugal and the EU were close. Legal uncertainty, data protection worries and the poor quality or availability of data were each cited by between 43% and 54% of EU firms, and by a few points more in Portugal. Few firms in either said AI was simply not useful to them, 18.2% in Portugal and 17.8% in the EU.

Two reasons stood apart. Cost was cited 22 percentage points more often in Portugal than across the EU. Ethical concerns were too, by 36.4% of Portuguese firms against 24.6% in the EU.

In 2026, INE's figures show cost still rising in Portugal, while most other reasons fell. A lack of skills dropped by nine points, and ethical concerns by nine. Medium-sized firms, with 50 to 249 staff, moved most: 72.3% of those that considered AI but held back cited cost, up from 55.3%.

The figures do not say what firms mean by cost: licences, equipment, new staff or time to adapt. They do show that, among Portuguese firms that looked at AI and stopped, money has become as big a barrier as know-how.

## What this does not claim

- That these are the reasons most Portuguese firms do not use AI. The question goes only to firms that considered AI, 13.2% of all firms in 2026.
- A comparison with the EU for 2026. Eurostat has not yet published it.
- That shifts of a few points within Portugal are meaningful on their own. The group asked is a small share of a sample survey.
- Comparisons by sector. In 2026 INE moved the survey to a new classification of economic activities.
