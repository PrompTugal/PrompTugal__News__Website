**Analysis.** This piece reads official documents and explains what they show. Every figure is linked to its source below.

## In short

- Portugal had about 60 megawatts (MW) of computing capacity in 2025, by the Government's count. A megawatt is a unit of electrical power, here the power the country's computers for AI and other heavy work can draw. Demand was about 170 MW.
- The Government aims for about 700 MW by 2030 in its middle scenario and about 1,000 MW in its fast one. It expects demand of about 980 MW.
- One private campus in Sines says it has 37.5 MW running and 1.2 gigawatts (GW), or 1,200 MW, planned in all.

## The story

The figures come from the National AI Agenda, which the Government approved in January 2026. AI models are trained and run on computers housed in data centres, buildings full of servers with their own power and cooling.

Its own numbers set out the gap. In 2025, demand was nearly three times the capacity in the country. The Agenda expects demand to grow by 41% a year in Portugal, against 21% across Europe, driven by generative AI, companies going digital and research.

Of the Government's two scenarios, its planned paths to 2030, only the fast one closes that gap. The middle one would leave 2030 demand about 280 MW short.

The Agenda describes the jump as "between 10 and 15 times". Its own figures give more: about 12 times for the middle target and about 17 for the fast one.

In April 2026 the Government added a National Data Centre Plan. It gives no new megawatt figures. It says installed capacity in Portugal "practically doubled" between 2020 and 2025 but stays "significantly below" the European average. It blames a "low rate" of projects actually built on long and bureaucratic processes, unclear procedures, limits on access to the electricity grid and scattered demand. It also says the grid has room for new data centres in the short term.

Sines, on the Alentejo coast, is where the largest private project sits. Start Campus, its developer, says the live capacity is its first building, SIN01, and that a second, SIN02, is planned at 200 MW. Its full plan is larger than the Government's 2030 target for the whole country. The company describes the campus as built for AI, cloud services and high-performance computing, the very large calculations used in science and engineering.

The two sets of figures cannot simply be added or compared. The Agenda does not say whether its megawatts are the power the computers use or the power a site draws from the grid. Start Campus counts the capacity of its campus without saying which. And the campus serves customers from anywhere, not only demand in Portugal.

## What this does not claim

- How much of any capacity runs AI. No public source splits AI from other cloud and computing work.
- That planned capacity will be built. The 2030 figures are Government targets, and Sines's 1.2 GW is the company's plan.
- That the scenarios are forecasts. They come from the Government, not an independent body.
- That Sines's capacity counts toward the national figures. Neither source says so.
