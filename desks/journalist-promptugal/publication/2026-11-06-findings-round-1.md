# Becoming a publication, round 1

For the pass's two open questions in the ideas ledger (PB-07, PB-22, PB-41): what Web Summit asks of media, and how a publication registers in Portugal. Every point has its source, read on 6 November 2026. No person's identity, address or document is in this file, as security@'s pre-review asks; where a step needs one, it says so and stops.

## 1. Web Summit's media accreditation

Web Summit's "Media accreditation policy" is published for Lisbon 2026 at <https://websummit.com/media/media-accreditation-policy/> (page titled "Web Summit | November 9-12, 2026"; the same text as its Vancouver copy). What it says:

- **Who it is for:** "journalists working for legitimate and suitable media outlets", validated by "proof of recently published work".
- **Who it excludes:** "marketing, advertising, or public relations firms, consultants or advisors, market researchers, brand content producers, employees of corporate content marketing or social media teams", and non-editorial staff of media outlets.
- **Who is assessed more closely:** "freelancers, contributors, analysts, filmmakers, crew, influencers, bloggers, YouTubers, or podcasters", who may be asked for proof of recent work with a media outlet, of assignment, of covering past Web Summit events, of pre-coverage of the coming event, and of coverage plans.
- **Afterwards:** Web Summit monitors the coverage accredited media produce; no coverage may mean no accreditation next time. Content sourced from Web Summit should acknowledge it.
- **Discretion:** the media team approves or denies through an online application, and can cancel accreditation at any time.

**What this means for PrompTugal.news (our reading):**

- **In our favour:** recently published work and pre-coverage of this edition both exist. There are 20 signed articles on the summit, its companies and its days (some by this desk, some by Vicky's desks), each sourced, and several of them are practical guides to the week (`content/articles/`).
- **The risk to answer before applying:** the "brand content producers" and "corporate content marketing" exclusions. Our own pages say PrompTugal.news is powered by RiskMandate, which the same team works on, "so we are not independent of RiskMandate" (`/about/`), and the participant disclosure calls it "not independent coverage" (`/about/participant.html`). An application should meet that head on: the editorial independence of what we publish, the sourcing rule, and the disclosure itself as evidence of good practice. synthetic@'s run 3 already found the disclosure's wording is what makes a reader hesitate.
- **Who applies:** a person. The policy accredits journalists, not agents, and the application asks for a person's details. That stays with Dinis and out of this repository.
- **What would strengthen it:** an editorial statute (see 2), a named editor in charge, a corrections policy as a page of its own (today it is a paragraph on the articles index), and a role contact address on promptugal.news, once Dinis says one may be published.

## 2. Registering a periodical publication in Portugal (ERC)

Portugal's media regulator, the ERC, registers periodical publications. The rules read are in Decreto Regulamentar n.º 8/99 of 9 June 1999, as ERC publishes it (<https://www.erc.pt/documentos/legislacaosite/DecretoRegulamentarn8_99.pdf>, read 6 November 2026):

- **Online is included.** The owners of a periodical publication "não podem iniciar a sua edição, mesmo electrónica, antes de efectuado o registo" (article 13: they may not start publishing, even electronically, before registering).
- **What the application needs (article 18, n.º 1):**
  - a synopsis of the editorial project, with its subject, its area of distribution and, for a news publication, a draft **editorial statute** ("projecto de estatuto editorial");
  - two copies of the title's logo;
  - the director's declaration accepting the post, with a copy of the director's identity document;
  - a declaration from INPI, the industrial property institute, that the title is not registered to someone else in the corresponding class.
- **Provisional, then definitive (article 15):** the registration is provisional and becomes definitive when the first issue is presented, within 90 days of approval.
- **Excluded (article 12):** publications not made available to the general public, and those of public bodies and of foreign diplomatic, cultural and trade missions. None of these applies to PrompTugal.news.
- **Fees:** the decree's own table is in escudos (1999). It has been amended since, and ERC publishes a current application form ("Requerimento para registo de publicação periódica"). Neither the current fee nor the form has been read for this round.

**Not answered, for Dinis and a lawyer:** whether a publication whose owner is The Cyber Boardroom, a UK company (at Dinis's word, ideas ledger PB-12), and which reports from Portugal must register with the ERC, or may. The decree is about periodicals published in Portugal, and this round did not establish how it applies to a foreign owner. Nothing here says PrompTugal.news is in breach of anything.
