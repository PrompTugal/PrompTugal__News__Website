# Draft: the editorial statute of PrompTugal.news

**Why this exists.** Portugal's Press Law asks every informative periodical for an editorial statute (*estatuto editorial*). The statute must "defina claramente a sua orientação e os seus objectivos": define clearly what the publication sets out to do. It must also commit the publication to the ethics of journalists and to "a boa fé dos leitores", the good faith of its readers (Lei n.º 2/99, article 17, n.º 1). The director writes it and the owner ratifies it (article 17, n.º 2; article 20). It is also the clearest answer to Web Summit's test of a "legitimate and suitable media outlet". What follows is a proposal for Dinis, who would be the director in the law's sense, and for The Cyber Boardroom, the owner. It describes only what PrompTugal.news already does, or what the board has decided it will do.

---

## Estatuto editorial / Editorial statute

**1. What PrompTugal.news is.** PrompTugal.news reports on technology and AI, in context, from Portugal for Europe. Its mandate is to map the universe of AI in Portugal. Its first edition covers Web Summit Lisbon 2026 (9 to 12 November): the companies that come, the people, the sessions and what happens there. It is published by The Cyber Boardroom, a UK company, and its editor-in-chief is Dinis Cruz.

**2. How it is made.** PrompTugal.news is written, checked and published by AI agents working under named people. Every agent has a written behaviour policy, published with the site. Every article is signed by the agent that published it, and the signature can be checked against our public keys. The editor-in-chief is responsible for everything we publish, including what no person read before it went live, and every article says which it is.

**3. Facts first, each with its source.** We publish only facts we can source. Each one links to a public page, with the date we read it. A company's claim is credited to the company. A figure we have not checked says so. Where sources disagree, we show the disagreement and do not pick a side we cannot support. Every article ends with what it does not claim. Our own suggestions and readings are marked as ours.

**4. People.** We publish nothing about a person that is not already public and professional, and never a contact detail. When unsure, we leave it out and say so.

**5. Independence, and our interest.** We are not affiliated with Web Summit and do not speak for it. PrompTugal.news is powered by RiskMandate, on which the same team works. So we are not independent of RiskMandate, and we say so on every page. We make no claim for RiskMandate beyond what riskmandate.ai says. No company pays to appear, and being covered is not an endorsement.

**6. Corrections and replies.** We correct mistakes on the page where they were made, with the date, the old text and what changed. We never quietly rewrite a published fact. Anyone may point out a mistake. A person or organisation we write about may ask for a reply or a correction, and we publish it as the law provides.

**7. Readers.** We keep the reader's good faith. We do not mislead about what a source says, who made a page, or what we know. We do not track readers with cookies or sell anything about them. What the site keeps about a reader is in our disclosure.

**8. Rights.** Our articles are published under CC BY 4.0, unless a page says otherwise.

**9. Changes.** Changes to this statute are published on this page with their date, and the earlier text stays in its public history.

## For Dinis (not part of the statute)

- **Who signs it.** The law has the director write it and the owner ratify it (article 17, n.º 2). That is Dinis as director, if he takes the role, and The Cyber Boardroom as owner. On the "conselho de redacção" (newsroom council) whose opinion article 17 also asks for: none exists. Article 19, n.º 4 waives the council's prior hearing only for the appointment of a first director, so whether one is needed is part of the ERC question on the decision page "For Dinis: registering PrompTugal.news".
- **What is new in it, and so needs your yes.** Point 2's line that the editor-in-chief is responsible for what no person read (what the articles say since 14:20 today). Point 5's "no company pays to appear". Point 6's right of reply "as the law provides" (articles 24 to 27 of the Press Law, not yet read for this draft). Point 7's "do not track readers with cookies", which the privacy-first pass must confirm, including the city lookup security@ flagged.
- **Where it goes.** A page, `/about/statute.html`, linked from About and every footer. Placing it is designer@'s and dev@'s, or this desk's if `content/about/` is added to its grant.
