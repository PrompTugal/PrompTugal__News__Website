## In short

- Three Portuguese startups announced rounds between 23 October and 3 November 2026, each to expand abroad, as reported by Portugal Startup News.
- [Granter](../organisations/granter/index.html), PITCH winner in 2025, raised 1.6 million euros for its AI grant platform and plans to grow into Spain, Germany and Italy.
- [Intuitivo](../organisations/intuitivo/index.html), PITCH winner in 2024, raised 1.35 million euros to take its assessment platform for teachers to Brazil.
- [Seamoretech](../organisations/seamoretech/index.html), a University of Porto spin-off, raised 1.1 million euros to recover freshwater and minerals from desalination waste, with a launch in Spain planned for 2027.

## The story

**Granter: an AI agent for grant applications.** Granter uses AI to help organisations find funding, prepare applications and manage approved projects. It has raised 1.6 million euros, in a round led by 3xP Global through its Impact Innovation Fund, under the Venture Capital Program of the Capitalization and Resilience Fund (FdCR), managed by Banco Português de Fomento. Celtis Venture Partners, Gateway Ventures and Deel also took part. With 400,000 euros raised earlier from Techstars, Mission and Angels Way, its total is 2 million euros. Granter plans to more than double its team by the end of 2026 and to expand into Spain, Germany and Italy; the United States is a medium-term priority, and Portugal remains its main market. Since winning PITCH in November 2025, it says it has helped more than 140 businesses apply for over 30 million euros. It is relaunching its AI Grant Consultant with a chat interface, in which organisations work with an AI agent; the company says tasks that took weeks can now be done in "under an hour".

**Intuitivo: time back for teachers.** Founded in Porto in 2021, Intuitivo helps teachers create, give and grade tests. It raised 1.35 million euros in seed funding, in a round led by Caixa Capital and the same 3xP Global Impact Innovation Fund, with Techstars, COREangels Lisboa, COREangels Porto and Angels Way. It counts more than 200 contracts covering more than 500 schools in Portugal and 75,000 registered teachers. Its AI drafts tests from a topic, year group and difficulty level, and AI-supported grading of open answers is planned for the 2026/27 school year. The money is for Brazil, where it estimates 2.2 million teachers, and to grow its team from 11 to 20 people by the end of 2026. João Guimarães, co-founder and CEO, said: "Our goal is simple: to give time back to those who teach".

**Seamoretech: water from brine.** Founded in 2021 as a spin-off of the University of Porto's Faculty of Engineering (FEUP), Seamoretech turns desalination brine into freshwater and minerals, using forward osmosis in containerised units. It raised 1.1 million euros in the first closing of its seed round, from Rypples, Seven Seas Capital and Crédito Agrícola, bringing its total to 3 million euros. It plans a commercial launch in Spain in 2027 and is building pilots in Spain and Egypt. Sofia Delgado, co-founder and CEO, said the aim is to "produce freshwater at a lower cost".

**What links them.** All three are Portuguese startups raising to sell abroad. Two of the three rounds share an investor: 3xP Global's Impact Innovation Fund, under the state development bank's FdCR programme, which led Granter's round and co-led Intuitivo's.

A suggestion: founders hoping to follow Granter and Intuitivo from PITCH to a funding round could look for both at the summit, and investors in water and cleantech could look up Seamoretech.

## What this does not claim

- **All figures are the companies' own**, from their press statements as reported by Portugal Startup News. PrompTugal.news has not checked them, including Granter's 140 businesses and 30 million euros, and Intuitivo's schools and teachers.
- **None of the three is confirmed at Web Summit 2026** in the sources read. The suggestion above is ours.
- **"Under an hour"** is Granter's claim for its AI agent. We have not tried the product.
- **The link through 3xP Global** is a shared investor, as the reports name it. It is not a claim that the companies work together.
