# Evidence and "details withheld"

**We say what we know, show how we know it, and say plainly what we have left out and why.**

## Two kinds of knowing

**That something exists.** An event is on, a company has opened an office, a council has a meeting. Often several sources point to it: a listing, a mention in a newspaper, a post by someone going. Each of those links is evidence, and we show them all, readable to us or not.

**What its details are.** The time, the place, the speakers, the price, the programme. These we publish only from a source we may read and whose terms let us use it. If the only source for a detail is one we do not read by machine, or one whose licence is not clear, the detail stays off the page.

## What a reader sees

Where details are missing for that reason, the page says so:

> **Details withheld.** We know this is happening from the sources linked below. Its details are published only on a site we do not read, or whose terms we have not cleared, so we do not repeat them here. If you run it, send us the details and we will add them, with you as the source.

The links are there, so you can go and read the details at their source yourself.

## What we do

1. **We look widely.** To find out what is on, we may ask a search tool that cites its sources. Nothing it says is published as such: what we keep is the links it gives.
2. **Every link we cannot read is a gap** in our sources, counted on our register: how many things point to it. A gap is something to fix on the record, by asking the source, not by working around it.
3. **We publish what we may**, with the evidence beside it, and the rest as "details withheld".
4. **We ask.** The organiser, the company or the council is invited to send the details. When they do, they become the source.

## What we never do

- **Never copy details from a source we do not read**, even when we know them from another route.
- **Never work around a refusal.** A site that tells our agents not to read it is not read, by us or by a tool on our behalf.
- **Never present a guess as a detail.** If a time or a place is not confirmed by a source we may use, it is not on the page.

## An example from our own record

Before 11 November, some of our articles gave times, rooms and speakers taken only from the Web Summit website, which we no longer read by machine. On 18 and 19 November, re-checking our articles claim by claim, we took those details off two of them and said so in a dated update on each; the events are still there, with what other sources confirm. A third article took its programme from the event's page on Luma, another site we no longer read by machine: it now says which details come only from that page and have not been re-checked.

## Why

A page that hides what it does not know looks more complete than it is. A page that says "we know this, from here, and we may not tell you that" lets you judge for yourself, and gives whoever holds the details a reason to publish them where everyone can use them.
