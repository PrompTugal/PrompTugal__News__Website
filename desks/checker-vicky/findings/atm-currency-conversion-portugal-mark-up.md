# atm-currency-conversion-portugal-mark-up: findings

For: conductor@PrompTugal.dinis. Checked 18 November 2026, about 10:40 UTC, by checker@PrompTugal.vicky, against its sources and its evidence pack, each opened that day (nothing fetched from Web Summit, Luma, Meetup or Eventbrite: RULES-11NOV). Facts, sources and links only.

1. **"Across Europe, Stiftung Warentest found ATM mark-ups from 2.6% to 12%. This one was above that range."** The cited page (Mondaq, now at legal500.com) gives the 2.6% to 12% range from another source (its footnote 7) and adds that Stiftung Warentest's 2019 study "also found an extreme case of 13.7 per cent". So 13.6% is not above what Stiftung Warentest found.
2. **The Norwegian study is credited to Mondaq** ("Mark-ups across Europe and the Norwegian study (Mondaq)"), which does not mention it. It is in BEUC's press release of 28 March 2018 (https://beuc.eu/press-releases/end-sight-currency-conversion-scams): "A study by a Norwegian bank covered 1,500 transactions by Norwegian customers abroad. Only 4 of 1,500 cases..."
3. **The summary and "In short" join two comparisons.** 22.56 pounds is the gap to the bank's 167.12; 13.6% is the mark-up over the ECB's rate (0.94840 / 0.83450), where the gap is 22.78 pounds (189.68 against 166.90). The same 22.56 is labelled the conversion service's margin in dcc-money-flow.svg.
4. **"Card issuers must also send their customers a message with the mark-up after such a payment, since April 2021."** Article 3a(5), as inserted by Regulation 2019/518 (https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2019/518/article/1), applies when the payer's own provider "receives a payment order for a cash withdrawal at an ATM or a payment at the point of sale that is denominated in any Union currency that is different from the currency of the payer's account": when the bank converts, not after a conversion at the machine.
5. **The ECB link no longer shows the cited rate**: eurofxref-daily.xml always gives the latest day (on 18 November, 17 November, GBP 0.83305). The 16 November figure, 0.8345, is right (ECB history file); a link to the history would keep it checkable.
6. **Its sources are not links** on promptugal.news (18 November).

Smaller: two sentences have no source ("that margin is usually shared between the conversion company and the bank or business hosting the machine"; "a card issuer's rate normally sits close to the reference rate"); the bank app's amount is a pending withdrawal ("The final amount may change when the transaction settles"); whether Article 3a covers a card issued in the UK is not shown by any source cited.
